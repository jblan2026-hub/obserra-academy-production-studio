## Cold open: the issue that remained a risk

A supplier delay was entered in the risk register six weeks ago. The delay has now occurred, but the record still says 70 percent probability. No issue owner, recovery action, escalation, or forecast change exists.

**NARRATOR**

A risk is an uncertain event or condition. An issue is a current condition requiring management. When uncertainty becomes reality, the project system must change from monitoring probability to managing impact and action.

6. Update plans and forecasts.

## Distinctions

### Risk

An uncertain event or condition that may affect objectives.

### Issue

A current event, condition, or problem affecting or requiring action.

### Impediment

A condition that slows, obstructs, or prevents progress.

### Blocker

An impediment that prevents specific work from continuing.

### Defect or nonconformance

A failure to meet an approved requirement or criterion.

### Decision

A choice requiring authorized judgment.

### Constraint

A limiting condition, such as a date, budget, resource, regulation, or physical boundary.

The response depends on the category and context. A missing executive decision is not solved through technical rework. A realized supplier delay should not remain only as a risk.

## Issue record

1. Identifier and date detected.
2. Facts and source.
3. Category.
4. Affected objectives, stakeholders, and deliverables.
5. Severity, urgency, and trend.
6. Immediate containment.
7. Owner and authority.
8. Cause and dependencies.
9. Options and selected action.
10. Schedule, cost, quality, risk, and benefit impact.
11. Escalation threshold.
12. Verification and closure evidence.

## Triage

Assess:

1. Safety and human impact.
2. Legal, regulatory, security, and contractual consequence.
3. Current operational harm.
4. Critical-path or milestone impact.
5. Cost and benefit impact.
6. Number of affected stakeholders.
7. Reversibility.
8. Time to act.
9. Evidence confidence.
10. Authority and dependency.

Urgency and severity are different. A low-severity decision may be urgent because a team is blocked. A severe issue may require careful containment before a permanent solution.

## Removing impediments

The project manager should help the team remove obstacles within authority and escalate those beyond it.

Common responses include:

1. Clarify a requirement or decision right.
2. Secure access, environment, or data.
3. Negotiate a resource or supplier commitment.
4. Change sequence.
5. Facilitate cross-team coordination.
6. Resolve process or policy ambiguity.
7. Automate repetitive waiting or control work.
8. Obtain an executive, legal, risk, or product decision.
9. Change scope or plan through governance.
10. Protect the team from unnecessary work and interruptions.

**ON SCREEN PRINCIPLE**

> Removing an impediment means changing the condition that blocks value, not merely moving the ticket to another owner.

## Root cause and recurrence

Issue closure should distinguish:

1. Immediate correction.
2. Containment.
3. Root-cause analysis.
4. Corrective action.
5. Preventive improvement.
6. Verification.
7. Lessons applied elsewhere.

A reopened issue may indicate incomplete cause analysis, ineffective action, changed conditions, or weak verification.

## Decision latency as an impediment

Unresolved decisions can become major schedule and quality constraints. Track:

1. Decision question.
2. Owner and authority.
3. Evidence needed.
4. Due date.
5. Work blocked.
6. Cost of delay.
7. Escalation path.
8. Decision and implementation.

A committee meeting cadence should not determine response time when the project impact requires an earlier decision path.

## Documented case: planning and execution impediments

GAO's propulsion-module case reported that fundamental planning and execution processes were not followed and that later assessment work clarified success criteria, assumptions, and alternatives.

The issue-management lesson is that repeated symptoms may require a change to the project system, not only a local fix.

**Limitations:** Historical government context; multiple technical and organizational contributors.

## Original scenario: Nimbus Environment Blocker

Nimbus is developing a regulated analytics product.

1. Integration testing is blocked because the shared environment is unavailable.
2. The environment owner says capacity is fully allocated for four weeks.
3. The critical-path release is in six weeks.
4. A temporary cloud environment is possible but requires security approval and additional cost.
5. The issue has been reassigned three times.
6. The sponsor believes the technical team should “work around it.”

**Strongest action:** Establish one issue owner, quantify critical-path and cost-of-delay impact, evaluate temporary environment, sequencing, resource, and release options, engage security and portfolio authority, make the required decision, and verify environment readiness.

The project obtains a controlled temporary environment, moves one test stream, updates cost and risk, and establishes an enterprise capacity action to reduce recurrence.

## Issue escalation

Escalate when:

1. Impact exceeds project authority or tolerance.
2. Safety, security, legal, ethical, or regulatory concerns require specialist ownership.
3. A critical decision is overdue.
4. Cross-project resource conflict cannot be resolved.
5. Supplier or contract action requires authorized procurement.
6. Corrective action repeatedly fails.
7. Stakeholder harm or material benefit loss increases.
8. The issue may require pause, rebaseline, or termination.

Escalation should include facts, impact, options, recommendation, authority, and required timing, not only “management attention needed.”

## Common exam traps

1. Leave a realized event in the risk register only.
2. Reassign an impediment without changing the blocking condition.
3. Escalate every issue immediately.
4. Delay escalation after authority is exceeded.
5. Close after correction without verifying corrective action.
6. Ask the team to work around a governance or resource problem indefinitely.
7. Hide issue impact to preserve status.

## Knowledge checks

1. A risk event occurs. **Manage it as an issue and execute or adapt the response.**
2. A team is blocked by an overdue executive decision. **Track decision impact and escalate through the defined authority path.**
3. A fix is applied repeatedly. **Investigate root cause and verify corrective action.**
4. An issue exceeds project authority. **Escalate with evidence, options, and timing.**

## Closing

Manage current reality. Classify the problem, contain harm, assign ownership, analyze cause, remove the blocking condition, use authority appropriately, update the forecast, and verify closure.
