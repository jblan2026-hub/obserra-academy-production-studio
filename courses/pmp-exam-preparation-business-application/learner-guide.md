# 1. What this course is designed to accomplish

The course provides 35 hours of structured project-management instruction aligned to the public July 2026 PMP Examination Content Outline. It prepares you to:

1. Understand the current examination architecture and eligibility paths.
2. Apply the People, Process, and Business Environment responsibilities in realistic situations.
3. Distinguish predictive, adaptive or agile, and hybrid contexts.
4. Interpret schedules, financial data, risk evidence, quality information, and stakeholder facts.
5. Select the best first or next action rather than a merely possible action.
6. Explain why a strong answer protects value, people, evidence, and governance.
7. Apply the same knowledge to real workplace projects.
8. Complete an original 180-question protected simulation and build a targeted remediation plan.

The course is not a shortcut around experience. PMP questions are designed to test applied judgment. Memorizing terminology without understanding sequence, authority, context, and value is not sufficient.

# 3. Eligibility and the 35-hour record

The official outline currently describes four education-and-experience paths. Depending on education, a candidate may need 60, 48, 36, or 24 months of non-overlapping project leadership experience within the preceding ten years. The current outline also generally requires at least 35 hours of commercial project-management training aligned to the examination outline, unless an applicable prior-learning path applies.

## Non-overlapping experience

Overlapping projects do not create duplicate calendar months. If you led Project A and Project B during March, March remains one month of experience. Keep a timeline that can be independently understood.

## Training evidence to retain

Retain:

- Your name and learner identifier.
- Course title and provider.
- Course objectives and syllabus.
- Actual instructional duration.
- Completion date.
- Assessment and passing record.
- Certificate or transcript.
- Version of the course and examination outline.
- Any correspondence needed to explain the record.

Books and practice examinations alone are not the same as the 35 hours of structured training described in the official outline. This course records lesson completion and protected assessment completion, but PMI retains authority over application and audit decisions.

# 4. How to use the learning environment

Each instructional module follows a repeatable sequence.

1. **Watch the lesson video.** Use captions and chapter markers. Pause when a source card or calculation requires review.
2. **Read the source notes.** Confirm whether the statement is an official requirement, professional guidance, public case fact, or instructor interpretation.
3. **Work the scenario.** Decide before viewing the explanation.
4. **Complete the workbook artifact.** Translate the concept into something a project leader could use.
5. **Use the AI tutor.** Ask for a different explanation, another original scenario, or workplace application.
6. **Complete retrieval practice.** Answer without notes before reviewing the rationale.
7. **Record confidence.** Confidence should track accuracy. High confidence with low accuracy requires immediate remediation.
8. **Complete the graded checkpoint.** The AI tutor locks while a protected assessment is active.
9. **Review the evidence.** Study why the best answer is strongest and why the distractors are weaker.
10. **Schedule spaced review.** Revisit weak concepts after a delay, not only immediately after instruction.

# 5. The Obserra decision lens

Use these six questions on every scenario.

## 1. Environment

What delivery environment is present?

- Predictive.
- Adaptive or agile.
- Hybrid.
- Unclear or insufficiently defined.

Do not assume that every team calling itself agile is operating adaptively. Look for actual evidence: product ownership, backlog management, iterative delivery, feedback, empowered teams, and tailoring.

## 2. Evidence

What fact is most important? What remains an assumption? What source, date, baseline, threshold, or stakeholder statement changes the answer?

## 3. Authority

Who has the authority to prioritize, accept, approve, change, escalate, or close? The project manager should act within authority and use the appropriate person or governance path when authority belongs elsewhere.

## 4. Sequence

What should happen first or next? Many distractors describe something that might eventually occur but skip diagnosis, engagement, analysis, approval, or planning.

## 5. Value and protection

Which response best protects:

- People and relationships.
- Customer and stakeholder value.
- Quality and compliance.
- Evidence and transparency.
- Schedule and financial integrity.
- Ethical conduct.
- Organizational governance.

## 6. Verification

How will you know the action worked? Closing a risk, resolving an issue, transferring knowledge, removing an impediment, or delivering a change requires evidence of outcome.

# 6. Common weak-answer patterns

Be cautious when an answer:

1. Acts before understanding the problem.
2. Escalates immediately when the team can still resolve the issue.
3. Avoids escalation when policy, authority, safety, ethics, or material impact requires it.
4. Bypasses the product owner, sponsor, customer, team, change authority, or governance body.
5. Accepts scope without analysis or authorization.
6. Changes a baseline informally.
7. Treats a risk as though it has already occurred.
8. Logs an issue but takes no action.
9. Replaces or disciplines someone before coaching and diagnosing the system.
10. Uses more reporting as a substitute for engagement.
11. Confuses completed output with realized value.
12. Applies a predictive control path to an adaptive decision without checking authority.
13. Assumes agile means no plan, documentation, governance, or compliance.
14. Assumes hybrid means using every method at once.
15. Ignores the need to verify the result.

# 8. Predictive, adaptive, and hybrid quick reference

## Predictive

Useful when the product and delivery path can be sufficiently defined, dependencies need coordinated baselines, contractual or regulatory control is strong, and change can be evaluated through formal impact analysis.

Typical artifacts:

- Charter.
- Integrated project-management plan.
- Scope statement.
- Work breakdown structure.
- Baseline schedule and cost baseline.
- Change requests and change log.
- Formal acceptance and closure records.

## Adaptive or agile

Useful when uncertainty is high, learning can occur through incremental delivery, feedback is frequent, and the product can evolve through prioritized work.

Typical artifacts:

- Product vision and product goal.
- Roadmap.
- Product backlog.
- Sprint or iteration goal.
- Definition of done.
- Increment review and retrospective results.
- Flow, cycle-time, or burn data.

## Hybrid

Useful when different parts of the work need different controls. For example, physical infrastructure may be planned predictively while a customer-facing application is developed iteratively. Hybrid requires explicit integration points, decision rights, shared milestones, evidence, and governance.

The correct answer is not the method with the most terminology. It is the method and action that fit the facts.

# 9. Quantitative reference

Use formulas as decision aids. Always state the assumptions.

## Earned value

- `CV = EV - AC`
- `SV = EV - PV`
- `CPI = EV / AC`
- `SPI = EV / PV`
- A common forecast when current cost performance is expected to continue: `EAC = BAC / CPI`
- `ETC = EAC - AC`
- `VAC = BAC - EAC`

Interpretation:

- A negative cost variance indicates that earned value is below actual cost.
- A CPI below 1.0 indicates unfavorable cost efficiency.
- A negative schedule variance indicates that earned value is below planned value.
- SPI is a value-based schedule indicator; it does not replace a network-schedule analysis.

## Three-point estimating

A commonly taught beta estimate is:

`Expected duration = (Optimistic + 4 × Most Likely + Pessimistic) / 6`

A simple triangular estimate is:

`Expected duration = (Optimistic + Most Likely + Pessimistic) / 3`

Use the formula specified by the scenario. Do not silently substitute one model for another.

## Communication channels

For `n` people, the number of potential pairwise channels is:

`n(n - 1) / 2`

This formula shows possible channels; it does not prove that every channel should be used or formally managed.

## Expected monetary value

`EMV = probability × impact`

Use positive values for opportunities and negative values for threats when the scenario uses that convention. EMV is an expected value across uncertainty, not a prediction of the exact outcome.

## Float and critical path

- Total float is the amount an activity may slip without delaying the project finish under the current model.
- The critical path is the longest-duration logical path through the schedule network under current assumptions.
- Resource constraints, calendars, leads, lags, and risk can change the critical path.

# 10. Public-case learning method

The course uses documented public cases to show how project conditions interact. Each case is treated through four lenses:

1. What the official source establishes.
2. What the source does not establish.
3. What project-management principles are relevant.
4. How the lesson transfers to another context.

Initial cases include:

- FBI Virtual Case File and Sentinel planning and oversight.
- NASA major-project cost and schedule risk.
- James Webb Space Telescope Joint Confidence Level analysis.
- GSA unforeseen construction conditions.
- UK Benefits Payment Card cancellation.

Do not reduce a complex project to “they should have used agile,” “the vendor failed,” or another single-cause explanation. Major projects involve governance, requirements, technical uncertainty, procurement, integration, people, incentives, oversight, external conditions, and decision quality.

# 14. Exam-day preparation

Before the exam:

1. Confirm the appointment, identification, modality, location, and current testing rules.
2. Test the system and room if taking an online proctored exam.
3. Sleep and eat in a manner that supports sustained concentration.
4. Stop using questionable or unfamiliar materials.
5. Review your pacing and break plan.
6. Review decision patterns, not hundreds of isolated facts.
7. Know how you will handle uncertainty and flagged questions.

During the exam:

1. Read the final question sentence carefully.
2. Identify the delivery approach and current project state.
3. Separate facts from assumptions.
4. Determine whether the question asks for first, next, best, or should-have-been-done.
5. Eliminate options that bypass people, evidence, authority, or governance.
6. Select, move on, and protect section pacing.
7. Review flagged items before ending the section.
8. Take the scheduled breaks if they support performance.

# 15. Readiness standard

A high practice score alone is insufficient. Strong readiness includes:

- Stable performance across all three domains.
- No critical ECO task blind spots.
- Comparable performance in predictive and adaptive or hybrid situations.
- Ability to interpret graphics and case evidence.
- Controlled pacing.
- Accurate confidence calibration.
- Ability to explain why distractors are weak.
- Ethical preparation.
- Ability to apply concepts to a real project.

A practice result is not an official PMI score and does not guarantee success. Use it as evidence for a study decision.
