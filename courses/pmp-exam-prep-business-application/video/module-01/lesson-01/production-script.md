## Scene 3 — The four eligibility pathways — 02:05–05:10

**VISUAL**  
A vertical education ladder appears in a dark 3D space. Four paths illuminate one at a time.

**NARRATOR**  
The July 2026 Examination Content Outline identifies four education-and-experience pathways. The required experience must be professional project leadership experience earned within the ten years before the application.

**PATH 1 — ON SCREEN**

- Upper-secondary education or equivalent
- Minimum 60 months, or five years
- Non-overlapping experience leading projects

**NARRATOR**  
Candidates using an upper-secondary credential generally need five years of non-overlapping experience leading projects.

**PATH 2 — ON SCREEN**

- Recognized associate-level, higher-certificate, or advanced technical or vocational program
- Minimum 48 months, or four years
- Non-overlapping experience leading projects

**NARRATOR**  
A recognized post-secondary short-cycle or technical pathway generally requires four years.

**PATH 3 — ON SCREEN**

- Recognized bachelor’s degree or higher, or a formally mapped bachelor-equivalent qualification
- Minimum 36 months, or three years
- Non-overlapping experience leading projects

**NARRATOR**  
A recognized bachelor’s-level or higher pathway generally requires three years.

**PATH 4 — ON SCREEN**

- Bachelor’s or postgraduate degree from a PMI Global Accreditation Center-accredited program
- Minimum 24 months, or two years
- Non-overlapping experience leading projects

**NARRATOR**  
Graduates of a qualifying GAC-accredited program generally use the two-year pathway.

**FACT CARD**

- Source: PMI-ECO-2026
- Scope: July 2026 PMP eligibility
- Observation date: 6 August 2026
- Limitation: PMI alone determines whether a credential and experience record are acceptable.

**NARRATOR**  
Do not infer eligibility from the name of a credential. A certificate described as advanced, professional, graduate, or executive is not automatically equivalent to a bachelor’s degree. The awarding system or competent authority must establish the level.

## Scene 4 — What counts as professional project leadership? — 05:10–07:50

**VISUAL**  
The frame divides into three moving columns:

1. Project leadership
2. Operational work
3. Personal or academic activity

**NARRATOR**  
The current outline describes a project as a temporary initiative in a unique context undertaken to create value. Qualifying experience is not simply time spent in a department or time holding a title with the word project.

**PROJECT LEADERSHIP — ON SCREEN**

- Planned and directed temporary work
- Made delivery decisions
- Led teams or stakeholders
- Managed outcomes, constraints, risk, or change
- Worked in a professional setting

**OPERATIONS — ON SCREEN**

- Repeated routine activity
- Ongoing service delivery with no temporary objective
- Administrative support without project leadership responsibility
- Work that cannot be separated from normal operations

**PERSONAL OR ACADEMIC — ON SCREEN**

- Home improvement
- Personal events
- Degree research completed solely as part of an academic program
- Informal activities outside a professional setting

**NARRATOR**  
A professional setting does not necessarily mean paid employment. It does mean the work must be professional and project-based. PMI directs applicants not to include routine operational or administrative tasks.

**SCENARIO A — MAYA**  
Maya ran weekly access reviews for two years. That work was important, but it was an ongoing operational control. It does not become a two-year project merely because she coordinated people and deadlines.

**SCENARIO B — MAYA**  
Maya also led a temporary identity-modernization initiative with a defined start, target state, budget, stakeholders, migration waves, risks, and transition to operations. That work is much more clearly project leadership experience.

**EXAM REASONING NOTE**  
On the exam and in business, first distinguish a project from ongoing operations. Then identify the temporary objective, the intended value, and the leader’s responsibility for decisions and outcomes.

## Scene 5 — Non-overlapping months: the most common counting mistake — 07:50–10:15

**VISUAL**  
A six-month timeline appears.

- Project A: January through April
- Project B: March through June

The months March and April glow red when someone attempts to count them twice.

**NARRATOR**  
Experience is counted in non-overlapping months, not by adding the duration of every project. In this example, Project A covers four months and Project B covers four months. But the calendar covers January through June only once. The total is six months, not eight.

**ON-SCREEN CALCULATION**

- Project A = 4 calendar months
- Project B = 4 calendar months
- Overlap = 2 months
- Eligible elapsed period represented = 6 non-overlapping months

**NARRATOR**  
The same principle applies when you lead three projects at once. Parallel responsibility may demonstrate complexity, but it does not multiply the passage of time.

**INTERACTIVE TIMELINE**  
The learner receives four project date ranges and must calculate the non-overlapping total. The system shows the union of calendar months and explains the result.

**AI TUTOR PROMPT**  
“Help me calculate my project months without storing employer names or sensitive project details.”

**PRIVACY NOTE**  
The tutor should accept date ranges and neutral project labels. It should not request confidential customer names, protected technical data, or employer secrets.

## Scene 6 — The 35-hour training requirement — 10:15–13:00

**VISUAL**  
A gold 35-hour ring fills clockwise. Around it appear icons for scope, schedule, communications, risk, procurement, finance, resources, stakeholders, integration, and governance.

**NARRATOR**  
In addition to the applicable education and experience pathway, PMP candidates generally need at least 35 hours of formal project-management training aligned to the topics in the Examination Content Outline, unless an eligible credit pathway applies.

**FACT CARD**

- At least 35 hours of project-management training
- Formal instruction aligned to the ECO
- Training completed before certification is granted
- Books and practice examinations alone do not satisfy the training requirement
- Training remains subject to PMI audit and quality review

**NARRATOR**  
The source outline lists several potential providers, including Authorized Training Partners, registered providers in China, GAC programs, accredited higher-education institutions, employer programs, training companies, consultants, and self-paced on-demand courses with an end-of-course assessment.

**ON-SCREEN DISTINCTION**

- **Training evidence:** instructional course, hours, objectives, provider, completion record
- **Study evidence:** books, notes, flashcards, practice exams

**NARRATOR**  
Study is essential, but study materials are not automatically training. Reading a book for twenty hours and taking fifteen hours of practice questions does not, by itself, create thirty-five formal training hours.

**OBSERRA COURSE STATEMENT**

This Obserra course is designed as 35 hours of self-paced project-management instruction aligned to the July 2026 ECO. It is independent training, not official PMI courseware. PMI alone decides whether training is accepted and may audit or reject low-quality or unsupported submissions.

**TIME-SENSITIVE NOTICE**

PMI has announced changes affecting live training providers in late Q4 2026. Self-paced training rules and all eligibility details must be re-verified before release and before a learner relies on them.

## Scene 7 — Build the audit file before you apply — 13:00–15:45

**VISUAL**  
A secure evidence vault opens. Five labeled records move into it.

1. Education evidence
2. Project experience inventory
3. Training certificate and syllabus
4. Provider and course details
5. Application narrative support

**NARRATOR**  
An audit-ready application begins before the application is submitted. The goal is not to manufacture a persuasive story. The goal is to preserve clear evidence of what actually occurred.

**APPLICATION EVIDENCE CHECKLIST — ON SCREEN**

- Official education record or accepted verification
- Project name or neutral identifier
- Start and end month
- Project objective and intended value
- Your leadership responsibilities
- Key outcomes and deliverables
- Organization or verifier information where required
- Training provider
- Course title
- Instructional hours
- Completion date
- Learning objectives or syllabus
- Completion certificate or transcript

**NARRATOR**  
Write your experience in project language, but do not inflate it. Describe decisions, leadership, planning, delivery, outcomes, and accountability. Do not rename operational work as a project, claim responsibility you did not hold, or hide overlapping dates.

**ETHICS FRAME**

PMI’s current ethics guidance identifies responsibility, respect, fairness, and honesty as central professional values. An application is the first professional-conduct test in the certification journey.

**ORIGINAL EXAMPLE — WEAK**

“I managed all technology for a global business transformation.”

**ORIGINAL EXAMPLE — STRONGER AND VERIFIABLE**

“Led the 11-month identity-migration workstream for a multi-region cloud transformation; planned migration waves, coordinated application owners, managed access and schedule risks, reported readiness, and transitioned the validated service to operations.”

**NARRATOR**  
The stronger description is specific without claiming more authority than the applicant actually held.

## Scene 8 — What the exam experience looks like — 15:45–18:30

**VISUAL**  
A cinematic exam interface appears without copying any real testing screen. Three domain arcs surround a central timer.

**FACT SEQUENCE**

- 180 total questions
- 170 scored questions
- 10 unscored pretest questions
- 240 minutes
- Multiple-choice, multiple-response, drag-and-drop, and practical or case-based formats may appear
- People 33%
- Process 41%
- Business Environment 26%
- Approximately 40% predictive
- Approximately 60% adaptive/agile and hybrid combined

**NARRATOR**  
The current examination contains 180 questions. Ten are unscored pretest questions used for future-exam validation, and they are mixed throughout the examination. Candidates do not know which questions are unscored.

The examination is scenario-based. The strongest answer often depends on what the project manager should do **next**, what authority exists, which stakeholder should be engaged, what evidence is missing, and whether the situation calls for analysis, collaboration, governance, escalation, or execution.

**ON-SCREEN WARNING**

Memorizing definitions is not enough.

**NARRATOR**  
The Examination Content Outline states that candidates must apply project-management concepts and experience to on-the-job situations. The PMBOK Guide is an important reference, but the ECO and the PMBOK Guide are not the same document and serve different purposes.

**PACING MODEL**

The course will teach a pacing plan, but no universal per-question timing guarantees success. Some interactive items take longer, while others take less time. The learner should practice completing full sections under realistic time constraints and reserve time for review.

**ORIGINAL KNOWLEDGE CHECK**

A candidate with a bachelor’s degree led Project A from January through May and Project B from April through September of the same year. Assuming all work otherwise qualifies, how many non-overlapping calendar months are represented?

A. 11 months  
B. 9 months  
C. 7 months  
D. 6 months

**PAUSE BEFORE REVEAL**

The learner selects an answer.

**RATIONALE**

The correct calculation is January through September, which represents nine non-overlapping calendar months. Adding five months and six months would double count April and May.
