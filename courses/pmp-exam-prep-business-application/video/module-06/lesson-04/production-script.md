## Cold open: delivered but not transitioned

A new identity-governance platform is declared complete. The project team disbands. Thirty days later:

1. No one owns access-review exceptions.
2. Supplier support contacts are unknown.
3. The risk register was archived with open items.
4. Operations did not receive recovery procedures.
5. The benefits dashboard has no owner.
6. Final invoices and contract obligations remain unresolved.
7. The certificate says complete.

**NARRATOR**

Delivery is not closure. Closure verifies that commitments, ownership, evidence, risk, knowledge, and value have been transferred or resolved under authority.

## Learning objectives

Learners will be able to:

1. Determine when a project or phase is ready to close.
2. Obtain authorized acceptance.
3. Transition product, service, control, risk, data, knowledge, suppliers, and benefits.
4. Close contracts and financial records through authorized roles.
5. Preserve lessons and records.
6. Manage premature termination.
7. Verify post-transition outcomes.

**Sources:** PMI ECO 2026, ISO 21502, GAO Schedule Guide, GAO Cost Guide, GAO VA EHR Incremental Improvement 2025, PMI Ethics.

## Closure criteria

A project or phase should have defined closure conditions for:

1. Scope and deliverables.
2. Acceptance evidence.
3. Quality and unresolved defects.
4. Security, safety, privacy, compliance, and accessibility.
5. Operational readiness.
6. Support and service ownership.
7. Knowledge transfer.
8. Supplier and contract obligations.
9. Cost, invoices, commitments, and assets.
10. Risk, issues, exceptions, and residual exposure.
11. Records and retention.
12. Benefits and outcome ownership.

## Acceptance and authority

Acceptance should identify:

1. Who is authorized to accept.
2. What is being accepted.
3. Which evidence demonstrates completion.
4. Which exceptions remain.
5. Who accepts residual risk.
6. Which ownership transfers occur.
7. Effective date and conditions.
8. What happens if conditions fail after transition.

The project manager coordinates acceptance but should not accept on behalf of an unauthorized stakeholder.

## Operational transition

A transition package may include:

1. Product and configuration baseline.
2. Architecture and design decisions.
3. Operating procedures.
4. Service levels and support model.
5. Monitoring and alerting.
6. Recovery, continuity, and incident procedures.
7. Access and identity controls.
8. Known defects, limitations, and workarounds.
9. Vendor, license, and renewal information.
10. Data ownership and retention.
11. Training and verified capability.
12. Change, release, and retirement authority.

**NARRATOR**

A document handoff is not proof of operational readiness. Use demonstrations, simulations, teach-back, supervised practice, and independent execution where appropriate.

## Residual risk and open work

Open risks and issues should not disappear at closure. Each should be:

1. Resolved.
2. Accepted by the authorized owner.
3. Transferred to a named operational or program owner.
4. Converted into governed follow-on work.
5. Escalated if no valid owner exists.

The closure report should state residual exposure, assumptions, monitoring, due dates, and review authority.

## Contract and financial closure

Authorized procurement, legal, finance, and contract roles may need to confirm:

1. Deliverable acceptance.
2. Final invoices.
3. Claims and disputes.
4. Warranties and support.
5. Intellectual-property and data obligations.
6. Asset transfer.
7. Security and access termination.
8. Retention and audit records.
9. Supplier-performance evaluation.
10. Exit assistance and remaining options.

The project manager should not declare a contract closed solely because technical delivery ended.

## Benefits transition

A benefit profile should transfer:

1. Benefit statement.
2. Baseline and target.
3. Data source and calculation.
4. Operational dependency.
5. Owner.
6. Timing.
7. Review cadence.
8. Corrective-action authority.
9. Assumptions and limitations.
10. Reporting destination.

**NARRATOR**

Benefits may emerge after the project closes. The receiving organization needs authority and capability to measure and act.

## Lessons learned and organizational knowledge

Lessons should capture:

1. Context and decision.
2. Evidence and assumptions.
3. What occurred.
4. Why it mattered.
5. What should be repeated, changed, or avoided.
6. Owner for process improvement.
7. Applicability limitations.
8. Evidence that the lesson was implemented.

A lesson recorded but never integrated into future work is historical commentary, not organizational learning.

## Premature termination

If the project is stopped before planned completion:

1. Confirm authorized termination.
2. Protect people, safety, security, data, and assets.
3. Stop or transition supplier commitments.
4. Preserve evidence and records.
5. Communicate to stakeholders.
6. Transfer or retire partial deliverables.
7. Resolve financial and legal obligations.
8. Capture lessons and remaining risks.
9. Release resources responsibly.
10. Define follow-on monitoring.

## Documented public context: paused deployments and ongoing transition evidence

GAO reported that VA paused electronic-health-record deployments and worked on incremental improvements, while updated life-cycle cost and integrated schedule evidence remained necessary. This demonstrates that a pause, transition, or restart decision should preserve current cost, schedule, user, risk, and readiness evidence.

**Limitations:** The federal healthcare program is complex and not a universal closure model.

## Original scenario: Aegis Platform Closure

Aegis has completed technical delivery.

1. Operations passed only tabletop training, not live failover.
2. Two critical risks remain open.
3. The supplier has not delivered data-export documentation.
4. Final acceptance is scheduled tomorrow.
5. The sponsor wants to release the team.
6. Benefits ownership is unassigned.

**Strongest action:** Present the closure-readiness evidence, identify unmet acceptance and transition conditions, assign authorized owners, complete or formally accept residual items, verify operational capability, and obtain the closure decision through governance.

Governance delays formal closure for two weeks, completes failover validation, assigns risk and benefit owners, resolves supplier evidence, obtains authorized acceptance, and then releases resources.

## Common exam traps

1. Close when all tasks are marked complete.
2. Transfer unresolved risk without an accepting owner.
3. Treat training attendance as readiness.
4. Archive lessons without assigning improvement actions.
5. Release the team before knowledge and contract closure.
6. Keep a failed project open because of sunk cost.
7. Assume acceptance transfers every legal, operational, or risk obligation.

## Knowledge checks

1. Deliverables are complete but operations cannot perform recovery. **Complete and verify transition before closure or obtain an authorized exception.**
2. A residual risk remains. **Transfer or accept it through a named authorized owner with monitoring.**
3. Benefits occur after closure. **Assign an operational benefit owner and review plan.**
4. A project is terminated. **Close it in a controlled manner rather than abandoning work and obligations.**

## Obserrian PMP Coach handoff

The tutor can build a closure checklist, transition plan, residual-risk handoff, benefit profile, or lessons-learned record. It must cite sources, ask about authority and obligations, and avoid legal or accounting determinations.

## Closing

Closure is an accountable transition, not an administrative ending. Verify acceptance, operational capability, residual risk, contracts, finance, knowledge, records, benefits, and authority. Preserve evidence and confirm who owns the outcome after the project team leaves.
