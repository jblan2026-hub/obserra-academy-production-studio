**VISUAL**  
One hundred eighty illuminated question nodes arrange into three horizontal sections of sixty. Ten nodes pulse differently but are not identified.

**NARRATOR**  
The public July 2026 outline specifies 180 total questions. One hundred seventy are scored. Ten are pretest questions used for future examination development. They are mixed into the examination, and the candidate cannot identify them.

**ON SCREEN**

- 180 total questions.
- 170 scored.
- 10 pretest.
- 240 minutes.
- Two scheduled ten-minute breaks.

After the first sixty nodes, a gold gate closes. A second gate closes after question 120.

**NARRATOR**  
The examination includes two scheduled ten-minute breaks. Once you finish review of a section and begin the break, you cannot return to that section.

**INTERACTIVE PAUSE**  
The learner chooses a strategy:

- Use all section time on first responses and leave no review reserve.
- Reserve controlled review time within each section.
- Spend as long as needed on every uncertain question.
- Rush Section 1 to create extra time later.

The system explains why controlled working time plus review time is the strongest starting plan.

## Scene 5 — Tasks and enablers — 08:25–10:45

**VISUAL**  
Each domain opens into task pathways. A task represents a responsibility. Enablers show examples of the work that demonstrates the responsibility.

**NARRATOR**  
The Examination Content Outline is organized by domains, tasks, and enablers. A task is not a memorization heading. It is a category of professional judgment. Enablers show the kinds of activities that may demonstrate the task.

**EXAMPLE**

Task: manage conflict.

Possible work includes:

- Identifying the source and stage of conflict.
- Understanding the context.
- Selecting a resolution strategy.
- Facilitating resolution.
- Reinforcing working agreements.

**NARRATOR**  
The exam may present those activities without naming the task. Your job is to recognize the professional responsibility inside the facts.

## Scene 6 — Delivery approaches across all domains — 10:45–13:25

**VISUAL**  
A 100-unit field appears.

- Approximately 40 units become predictive.
- Approximately 60 units become adaptive or agile and hybrid.

Those units flow through all three domains.

**NARRATOR**  
The public outline states that approximately 40 percent of the examination represents predictive approaches. The remaining approximately 60 percent is divided between adaptive or agile and hybrid approaches. The approaches appear throughout all three domains.

### Predictive indicators

- Sufficiently defined scope and solution.
- Baselines.
- Network schedule.
- Formal impact analysis.
- Contract or regulatory control.
- Formal acceptance and closure.

### Adaptive indicators

- Product goal.
- Prioritized backlog.
- Incremental delivery.
- Frequent feedback.
- Empowered cross-functional team.
- Retrospectives and flow measures.

### Hybrid indicators

- Different workstreams use different approaches.
- Shared milestones and governance.
- Explicit integration points.
- Delegated local decisions with common thresholds.

**NARRATOR**  
Do not answer from labels alone. A team may call itself agile while centralizing every decision and releasing once a year. Look for evidence of how the work is actually governed and delivered.

## Scene 7 — Original question formats — 13:25–15:55

**VISUAL**  
Original Obserra interface prototypes appear. They must not reproduce PMI examination screens.

### Single response

Select one strongest answer among plausible options.

### Multiple response

Evaluate each option independently and select the required number.

### Case or scenario

Use one evidence set for multiple questions. Separate stable facts from new facts introduced later.

### Graphic-based

Interpret schedules, data tables, burn charts, risk graphics, financial information, or stakeholder maps.

### Matching and enhanced matching

Connect roles, actions, categories, evidence, or response strategies.

### Point-and-click, sequencing, and drag-and-drop

Identify a location, order steps, or classify items. Accessible keyboard alternatives are required in this course.

**NARRATOR**  
The interface may change. The reasoning standard does not. Understand context, evidence, authority, sequence, value, and verification.

## Scene 8 — Pacing without panic — 15:55–18:45

**VISUAL**  
The 240-minute clock divides into three sections and two break gates. An average of 80 seconds per question appears, then becomes a flexible distribution.

**NARRATOR**  
Two hundred forty minutes divided by 180 questions equals an average of 80 seconds per question. That is a planning reference, not an official time limit for each item.

Some independent questions require less time. Graphic and case questions may require more. Your goal is to protect the entire section.

### Profile A — overinvestment

The learner spends five minutes on one uncertain item and loses review time.

### Profile B — rushing

The learner finishes early but repeatedly misses the words first, next, best, and should have done.

### Profile C — controlled pacing

The learner identifies low-confidence items, selects the best supported answer, flags when appropriate, and preserves review time.

**NARRATOR**  
Do not wait for certainty that may never arrive. Make the strongest decision supported by the evidence and protect the rest of the section.

## Scene 9 — The six-part decision lens — 18:45–21:10

**VISUAL**  
Six rings rotate over a project scenario.

### Environment

Predictive, adaptive, hybrid, or unclear?

### Evidence

What is known? What is assumed? What source, date, baseline, threshold, or stakeholder statement matters?

### Authority

Who may prioritize, accept, approve, change, escalate, or close?

### Sequence

Does the question ask for first, next, best, or what should have been done?

### Value and protection

Which response protects people, value, quality, compliance, evidence, ethics, and governance?

### Verification

What record, measure, test, acceptance, or observation proves the action worked?

**NARRATOR**  
Weak answers often jump from the problem directly to action. Strong answers preserve the decision sequence.

## Scene 10 — Scenario 1: Adaptive team conflict — 21:10–23:20

**SCENARIO**  
A product owner wants to release a high-value feature. The security architect identifies a control weakness. The team stops discussing the issue and waits for the project manager to decide.

**QUESTION**  
What should the project manager do first?

A. Overrule the product owner because security always wins.  
B. Facilitate analysis of the conflict, evidence, decision rights, and release constraints.  
C. Escalate immediately to the chief executive officer.  
D. Release now and record the weakness as technical debt.

**DECISION-LENS ANALYSIS**

- Environment: adaptive product work.
- Evidence: a weakness exists, but severity and constraints require analysis.
- Authority: product, security, and release authorities may differ.
- Sequence: diagnose and facilitate before premature override or escalation.
- Value and protection: protect customer value and security.
- Verification: document the decision, control disposition, and release evidence.

**CORRECT RESPONSE**  
B.

---

## Scene 11 — Scenario 2: Predictive critical dependency — 23:20–25:15

**SCENARIO**  
A critical vendor milestone is late, has no remaining float, and feeds system integration. The vendor promises recovery but provides no schedule, resource, or quality evidence.

**QUESTION**  
What should the project manager do next?

A. Accept the promise and maintain green status.  
B. Validate the recovery plan, analyze schedule impact, update risk and issue records, and use contract governance.  
C. Add internal resources to the vendor activity without agreement.  
D. Wait until the integration milestone is missed.

**CORRECT RESPONSE**  
B.

**NARRATOR**  
The missed vendor milestone is an issue. Future integration effects remain risks. The project manager needs schedule evidence, contract governance, and a verified recovery path.

**SOURCE BRIDGE**  
GAO-SCHEDULE-GUIDE supports the practical importance of integrated schedule logic, status, critical path, and risk analysis. It does not define a live PMP examination answer.

## Scene 12 — Scenario 3: External compliance change — 25:15–27:05

**SCENARIO**  
A regulator publishes a requirement that may affect a product under development. The team proposes immediate code changes. Applicability has not been confirmed.

**QUESTION**  
What should the project manager do first?

A. Implement immediately because every regulatory publication is mandatory.  
B. Confirm applicability with qualified owners and analyze scope, design, evidence, risk, and change impacts.  
C. Ignore the publication until an audit.  
D. Ask the supplier to determine the organization’s legal obligations.

**CORRECT RESPONSE**  
B.

**NARRATOR**  
Urgency does not remove the need for applicability and authority. The project manager integrates qualified compliance input, impact analysis, and change governance.

## Scene 13 — Scenario 4: Output without value — 27:05–28:55

**VISUAL DATA**

- Planned scope delivered: 100 percent.
- Schedule: on time.
- Budget: within tolerance.
- User adoption: 38 percent against a 75 percent target.
- Operating-cost reduction: not realized.
- Customer effort: increasing.

**QUESTION**  
What should the project manager do?

A. Declare success because all scope was delivered.  
B. Review benefits, adoption, assumptions, and transition evidence with the benefits owner and sponsor.  
C. Add more features without analysis.  
D. Remove adoption from the report because it occurs after delivery.

**CORRECT RESPONSE**  
B.

**NARRATOR**  
Output is not the same as value. The project manager connects delivery evidence to outcome evidence and accountable benefit ownership.
