## Scene 5. Contract approaches and risk allocation, 10:30 to 15:15

**VISUAL**

A continuum shows cost and performance risk moving between buyer and seller.

### Fixed-price approaches

Best suited when requirements can be defined sufficiently and the supplier can reasonably price the risk.

**FAR CONTEXT CARD**

In United States federal acquisition, FAR Part 16 states that a firm-fixed-price contract places maximum risk and full responsibility for costs and resulting profit or loss on the contractor.

**NARRATOR**

Fixed price does not eliminate buyer risk. Poor requirements, uncontrolled change, weak acceptance, supplier failure, or unrealistic pricing can still damage the project.

### Cost-reimbursement approaches

The buyer reimburses allowable costs and may pay a fee. These approaches may fit uncertain research or development when work cannot be estimated with sufficient confidence, but they generally require stronger cost oversight and place more cost risk on the buyer.

### Time-and-materials or labor-hour approaches

Payment is based on labor time and materials. They can provide flexibility but require clear ceilings, oversight, rate controls, and transition planning.

### Incentive approaches

Fees or price adjustment may be linked to cost, schedule, quality, performance, or outcomes. Incentives should avoid encouraging behavior that harms another objective.

### Indefinite-delivery or framework approaches

Provide a mechanism for future orders when exact quantities or timing are uncertain, subject to the applicable procurement system.

**NARRATOR**

These are general educational distinctions. Legal definitions and permitted uses vary by jurisdiction and contract.

10. Support and exit.
11. Cultural and strategic fit.

**NARRATOR**

Evaluation criteria should be defined before reviewing proposals and applied fairly.

In United States federal acquisition, FAR Subpart 42.15 establishes policy for contractor performance information and its relevance to future source selection. Commercial organizations may use different processes, but the principle is transferable: past performance should be supported by relevant evidence, context, and fair evaluation.

**PMI ETHICS CONNECTION**

Fairness requires consistent criteria and disclosed conflicts of interest. Honesty requires accurate evaluation records. Responsibility requires preserving procurement integrity and decision evidence.

## Scene 8. Supply-chain and fourth-party risk, 21:35 to 25:00

**VISUAL**

A primary supplier expands into components, cloud services, subcontractors, data processors, open-source dependencies, logistics providers, and support organizations.

**NARRATOR**

Supplier risk extends beyond the contracted company.

CISA supply-chain guidance treats information and communications technology supply-chain risk as a lifecycle concern involving products, services, suppliers, dependencies, vulnerabilities, malicious functionality, counterfeits, and disruption.

NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 includes supply-chain risk management within the Govern function and supports defining roles, requirements, risk processes, and monitoring.

A project procurement plan should consider:

1. Critical sub-suppliers.
2. Geographic and geopolitical exposure.
3. Single-source components.
4. Software and data dependencies.
5. Supplier access and identity.
6. Vulnerability and incident notification.
7. Change and component provenance.
8. Continuity and alternate sources.
9. Exit and recovery.

**LIMITATION CARD**

CISA and NIST guidance is cybersecurity-focused and voluntary unless adopted through law, contract, policy, or another obligation. Tailor it to the project.

5. Service and support.
6. Security and compliance evidence.
7. Risk and incidents.
8. Change requests.

11. Forecast and confidence.
12. Renewal or exit recommendation.

**NARRATOR**

Supplier performance should be measured against the agreement and project outcome.

The project manager should not direct out-of-scope work informally. Changes should follow authorized contract and project processes.

When performance weakens:

1. Verify facts.
2. Review contract and acceptance terms with authorized specialists.
3. Identify cause and impact.
4. Engage supplier leadership.
5. Agree corrective action and evidence.
6. Apply remedies through authorized roles.
7. Update project forecasts and risks.
8. Prepare continuity or exit when necessary.

## Scene 10. Original enterprise scenario: Solstice AI Vendor, 27:45 to 31:55

**LABEL**

Obserra original synthetic scenario. Not a documented public case.

**SCENARIO**

Solstice is selecting a generative AI supplier for customer operations.

1. Supplier A has the lowest subscription price.
2. Supplier B costs 20 percent more but provides stronger data controls and audit evidence.
3. Supplier C offers a custom model under a cost-reimbursement development arrangement.
4. The project has uncertain integration requirements.
5. Customer data may include sensitive information.
6. The business wants launch in four months.
7. Exit, model-update, subcontractor, and data-return requirements are not yet defined.

**QUESTION**

What should the project manager do next?

**OPTIONS**

A. Select Supplier A because price is lowest.  
B. Select Supplier B because security is always the highest priority.  
C. Complete the procurement strategy and evaluation criteria, define data, security, performance, change, subcontractor, acceptance, and exit requirements, assess contract and supplier risk with authorized specialists, and conduct a governed source selection.  
D. Begin work with all three suppliers before contracting.

**NARRATOR**

Option C is strongest. The project needs a defined acquisition and risk model before selecting a supplier.

**FOLLOW UP**

The organization chooses a staged pilot with a supplier that meets data and security gates. The agreement defines model changes, audit evidence, incident notification, service levels, data return, termination assistance, and a controlled expansion decision.

## Scene 11. Common PMP procurement traps, 31:55 to 33:45

### Trap 1. Choose the lowest price

Evaluate life-cycle value, performance, risk, quality, support, and exit.

### Trap 2. Use fixed price for highly uncertain work without analysis

Align contract type and risk allocation to the work and market.

### Trap 3. Direct supplier work informally

Use authorized contract channels.

### Trap 4. Accept delivery without evidence

Apply defined acceptance criteria and independent verification where required.

### Trap 5. Ignore subcontractors and supply chain

Understand critical dependencies and fourth-party exposure.

### Trap 6. Wait until termination to plan exit

Define portability, data return, knowledge transfer, continuity, and assistance before commitment.

## Knowledge check package

### Question 1

Requirements are highly uncertain and the buyer retains significant design control. What should the project manager do?

A. Select a contract type by habit.  
B. Work with procurement and legal specialists to analyze scope maturity, risk allocation, incentives, oversight, and permitted contract approaches.  
C. Promise fixed price without supplier input.  
D. Start work without an agreement.

**Correct response:** B.

### Question 2

Which procurement factor most directly addresses vendor lock-in?

A. Logo design.  
B. Data portability, intellectual-property rights, transition assistance, and exit terms.  
C. Meeting frequency.  
D. Office location only.

**Correct response:** B.

### Question 3

A supplier misses a milestone. What should the project manager do first?

A. Terminate immediately.  
B. Verify evidence, review impact and contract terms with authorized roles, and engage the supplier on corrective action.  
C. Ignore the delay.  
D. Direct uncontracted overtime.

**Correct response:** B.

### Question 4

Why should fourth-party dependencies be analyzed?

A. The contracted supplier may rely on critical products, services, or subcontractors that affect project risk and continuity.  
B. Every subcontractor must report directly to the project manager.  
C. Contracts eliminate supply-chain risk.  
D. Price is the only relevant factor.

**Correct response:** A.
