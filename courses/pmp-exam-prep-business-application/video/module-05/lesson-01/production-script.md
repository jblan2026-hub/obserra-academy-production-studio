**NARRATOR**

Headcount is not capacity. Assignment is not availability. Availability is not competence. Competence is not authority.

Resource management ensures that people, equipment, facilities, data, environments, materials, suppliers, funding, and decision capacity are available when the project needs them.

## Scene 4. Capacity and allocation, 06:45 to 10:10

**VISUAL**

A weekly capacity calculation appears.

One specialist has 40 working hours.

Subtract:

1. 6 hours of operational duty.
2. 4 hours of mandatory training.
3. 5 hours of approved leave.
4. 3 hours of recurring governance.
5. 4 hours of support and unplanned work allowance.

Available planned project capacity:

> 40 minus 6 minus 4 minus 5 minus 3 minus 4 = 18 hours.

**NARRATOR**

A nominal 40-hour assignment may provide only 18 hours of planned capacity.

Capacity planning should include:

1. Productive availability.
2. Nonproject obligations.
3. Learning and coordination.
4. Interruptions and variability.
5. Skill level and rework.
6. Time-zone and handoff effects.
7. Sustainable workload.

**LIMITATION CARD**

Do not treat every hour as interchangeable. Creative, analytical, physical, safety-critical, and highly coordinated work may have different capacity patterns.

## Scene 6. Bottlenecks and critical resources, 13:15 to 16:10

**VISUAL**

A resource network shows one specialist controlling several critical activities.

**NARRATOR**

A critical resource is a person, capability, environment, supplier, facility, or decision channel whose constraint can materially affect project outcomes.

Indicators include:

1. One qualified person.
2. Long replacement lead time.
3. High switching cost.
4. Shared demand across projects.
5. Required approval authority.
6. Specialized access.
7. Concentrated knowledge.
8. No tested backup.

The project manager should consider:

1. Cross-training.
2. Sequencing.
3. Protected allocation.
4. Backup capability.
5. Contract support.
6. Automation.
7. Early decision windows.
8. Risk response and contingency.

**ON SCREEN WARNING**

> High utilization can reduce flow, increase queueing, hide risk, and eliminate recovery capacity.

## Scene 7. Resource smoothing and leveling, 16:10 to 19:10

**VISUAL**

One specialist is over-allocated across three activities.

### Smoothing

Moves two noncritical activities within available float. The project completion date does not change.

### Leveling

Changes activity timing to resolve the conflict when float is insufficient. The critical path or completion date may change.

**NARRATOR**

The project manager should analyze the effect on:

1. Critical path.
2. Milestones.
3. Cost.
4. Quality.
5. Risk.
6. Stakeholder commitments.
7. Supplier work.
8. Benefit timing.

**EXAM TRAP**

Do not assume that over-allocation can be solved through overtime. Overtime may create fatigue, errors, morale problems, safety concerns, and unsustainable cost.

## Scene 9. Guidance-based evidence: schedule and cost integration, 22:20 to 25:10

**SOURCE CARD**

U.S. Government Accountability Office  
GAO Schedule Assessment Guide, GAO 16 89G  
GAO Cost Estimating and Assessment Guide, GAO 20 195G

**NARRATOR**

GAO guidance emphasizes resource-loaded schedule logic and cost estimates that reflect the technical baseline, work, risk, and actual performance.

A project forecast cannot be credible if it assumes resources that are not available or omits the cost of acquiring, developing, or sustaining capability.

**LIMITATIONS CARD**

Government acquisition guidance should be tailored to project context. It does not prescribe one staffing model or utilization target.

## Scene 10. Original enterprise scenario: Phoenix Cyber Transformation, 25:10 to 29:40

**LABEL**

Obserra original synthetic scenario. Not a documented public case.

**SCENARIO**

Phoenix is implementing identity governance across the enterprise.

1. The project is on the critical path for regulatory readiness.
2. One identity architect is assigned 30 percent to Phoenix and four other projects.
3. The schedule assumes 80 percent availability.
4. The integrator can provide an architect in six weeks at additional cost.
5. The internal architect holds critical organizational knowledge.
6. The sponsor wants the original date preserved.
7. Quality defects are increasing as the team accelerates.

**QUESTION**

What should the project manager do next?

**OPTIONS**

A. Require the internal architect to work overtime.  
B. Keep the plan unchanged until the milestone is missed.  
C. Validate actual capacity and skill needs, analyze sequence, external support, cost, knowledge-transfer, quality, and schedule options, then obtain the authorized portfolio and sponsor decision.  
D. Replace the architect immediately.

**NARRATOR**

Option C is strongest. The project manager should expose the capacity assumption and present executable options.

**FOLLOW UP**

Governance protects 60 percent of the internal architect's time, contracts short-term external support, pairs the two architects for knowledge transfer, defers one low-value integration, and updates cost, schedule, risk, and quality forecasts.

The project does not pretend the original resource assumption remains true.

## Scene 12. Common PMP resource traps, 31:45 to 33:25

### Trap 1. Add people immediately

First understand skill, onboarding, coordination, timing, and work divisibility.

### Trap 2. Treat assignment as availability

Validate actual capacity and competing work.

### Trap 3. Solve every conflict with overtime

Analyze sustainability, quality, safety, and cost.

### Trap 4. Protect one project without portfolio authority

Escalate competing demand through the authorized portfolio or functional process.

### Trap 5. Ignore nonhuman resources

Model environments, equipment, data, facilities, suppliers, and decision capacity.

### Trap 6. Maximize utilization

Protect flow, recovery, risk response, and learning capacity.

## Knowledge check package

### Question 1

A specialist has 40 working hours but 22 hours of other approved obligations. What is the planned project capacity before additional variability?

A. 40 hours.  
B. 22 hours.  
C. 18 hours.  
D. 62 hours.

**Correct response:** C.

### Question 2

A resource conflict cannot be resolved within float. Which technique may change the critical path or completion date?

A. Resource leveling.  
B. Resource smoothing only.  
C. Status reporting.  
D. Acceptance testing.

**Correct response:** A.

### Question 3

Five projects need one specialist. What should a project manager do?

A. Secretly reserve the specialist.  
B. Present value, risk, timing, alternatives, and impact through the authorized portfolio or functional decision process.  
C. Assume the specialist can multitask.  
D. Remove resource data from the schedule.

**Correct response:** B.

### Question 4

Which statement best describes resource availability?

A. A person's name appears in the plan.  
B. The project can use the required capability at the required time under the required conditions.  
C. The person works for the organization.  
D. The person attended the kickoff.

**Correct response:** B.

## Closing summary, 33:25 to 34:35

**NARRATOR**

Resource management connects work to real capacity, capability, timing, authority, cost, and resilience.

Validate availability. Identify critical skills and nonhuman constraints. Resolve over-allocation through smoothing, leveling, sequencing, support, scope, or governance. Protect sustainable performance and knowledge continuity.

On the PMP exam, do not solve every resource problem by adding people or overtime. Diagnose the constraint, analyze integrated impact, engage the authorized owners, update the plan, and verify the result.

**ON SCREEN**

> Plan real capacity. Protect critical capability. Govern competing demand. Sustain performance.
