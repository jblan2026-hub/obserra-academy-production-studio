# Sequence 1 — Cold Open: The Audit Notice

### Picture

Black screen. A low, controlled pulse begins. Gold particles trace the outline of the Obserra mark, then dissolve into a cinematic night view of a city. A project command center glows inside a high-rise office. Screens show a global transformation program: schedules, budget, stakeholders, risk, and benefits.

Cut to a candidate at a desk. We do not show a real PMI portal or recreate a proprietary screen. An original interface displays:

> APPLICATION SELECTED FOR AUDIT

The candidate’s expression changes. The camera moves through the submitted evidence as a three-dimensional timeline.

- Project Atlas: January–June.
- Project Horizon: March–August.
- Project Meridian: May–October.

The overlapping months glow red as if they were counted more than once.

A second panel shows:

- “35 hours claimed.”
- “Practice exam package.”
- “No instructional ledger.”
- “No learning objectives.”
- “No completion evidence.”

The interface stops. All sound drops except one clock tick.

### Narrator

“An application is not a résumé headline. It is an evidence statement. Every month, every training hour, and every claim must be accurate enough to withstand review.”

**What evidence should have existed before this application was submitted?**

### Interactive pause

The learner selects all that apply:

- A non-overlapping experience timeline.
- A structured training record.
- Learning objectives aligned to the current examination outline.
- Completion and assessment evidence.
- A promise from a trainer that PMI will accept the course.

The first four are retained. The promise fractures and disappears.

# Sequence 3 — The Four Eligibility Paths

## Timecode 04:10–08:00

### Picture

Four illuminated doors appear in a long architectural hall. Each door represents one current public eligibility path. The hall is labeled `CURRENT PUBLIC REQUIREMENTS — VERIFY BEFORE APPLYING`.

The production must not imply that the course makes official eligibility determinations. Each path includes a visible `PMI CONTROLS ACCEPTANCE` banner.

### Door 1

- Secondary diploma or equivalent.
- 60 months of non-overlapping project leadership experience.
- Experience within the preceding ten years.
- 35 hours of project-management training, unless a current applicable exception applies.

### Door 2

- Recognized short-cycle or technical-vocational post-secondary credential.
- 48 months of non-overlapping project leadership experience.
- Experience within the preceding ten years.
- 35 hours of project-management training, unless a current applicable exception applies.

### Door 3

- Bachelor’s degree or higher.
- 36 months of non-overlapping project leadership experience.
- Experience within the preceding ten years.
- 35 hours of project-management training, unless a current applicable exception applies.

### Door 4

- Bachelor’s degree or higher from a PMI Global Accreditation Center-accredited program.
- 24 months of non-overlapping project leadership experience.
- Experience within the preceding ten years.
- GAC core project-management coursework is preapproved for the 35-hour requirement under the current outline.

### Narrator

“The current outline describes four pathways. Education changes the experience requirement, but every path is an evidence path. The candidate must identify the applicable route, demonstrate non-overlapping project leadership experience, and retain training evidence.”

### Visual demonstration — non-overlapping months

A ten-year calendar fills the screen. Three project bars overlap.

- Atlas: January through June.
- Horizon: March through August.
- Meridian: May through October.

The calendar counts January through October once: ten calendar months, not eighteen project-month entries.

### Narrator

“Parallel leadership may demonstrate complexity, but it does not duplicate time. If two projects occur in March, March is one month.”

### Learner interaction

The learner receives four sample projects with overlapping dates and calculates the eligible calendar months. The system provides feedback only after submission.

### Source card

- PMI-ECO-2026
- Eligibility paths and non-overlapping experience
- Retrieved for course verification: August 7, 2026

# Sequence 4 — What Counts as a Project Experience Record

## Timecode 08:00–10:50

### Picture

A generic résumé dissolves into a project evidence table. Vague statements are highlighted:

- “Managed operations.”
- “Helped with systems.”
- “Led many initiatives.”

The table rebuilds each statement into project evidence:

- Temporary initiative.
- Unique product, service, result, or change.
- Objective and business context.
- Candidate leadership responsibility.
- Dates.
- Outcome or transition.
- Evidence and verifier.

### Narrator

“A strong experience record separates projects from routine operations. It describes the temporary initiative, the unique outcome, the leadership responsibility, the dates, and the result. It does not rely on title alone.”

### Original example

A cybersecurity leader ran a permanent vulnerability-management function and also led a nine-month enterprise implementation of a new risk-prioritization platform.

The permanent function is ongoing operations. The implementation is a temporary project if it had a defined objective, time boundary, leadership work, delivery, and transition.

### Narrator

“Do not transform operations into projects by changing the wording. Accuracy matters.”

### Ethics overlay

- Responsibility.
- Respect.
- Fairness.
- Honesty.

### Source card

- PMI-ETHICS-2025
- PMI ethics guidelines and professional conduct expectations

“Training must be more than a timer. It should have objectives, instruction, activities, assessment, and completion evidence. PMI may audit education and may evaluate the quality of training.”

4. PMI may audit and may deem poor-quality training ineligible.

# Sequence 6 — Ethics, Exam Dumps, and Professional Identity

## Timecode 15:00–19:30

### Picture

A study group meets in a virtual room. A participant uploads a file labeled:

> REAL JULY 2026 PMP QUESTIONS — RECONSTRUCTED

The file pulses red. The learner’s cursor approaches but stops.

### Narrator

“Someone says the questions were reconstructed from memory. Everyone else is using them. What should you do?”

### Interactive decision

Options:

1. Use the file but do not share it.
2. Open it only to compare it with official materials.
3. Decline the file, avoid distribution, and continue with authorized or original practice.
4. Memorize the questions, then delete the file.

After selection, the scene rewinds and shows the consequences of each weak option.

### Narrator

“The strongest response is to decline and avoid using or distributing questionable content. Exam integrity is not separate from project leadership. A person willing to misrepresent exam preparation may also be tempted to hide a schedule variance, minimize risk, or overstate benefits.”

### Picture

Four pillars rise:

- Responsibility.
- Respect.
- Fairness.
- Honesty.

A fifth translucent ring surrounds them:

- Accountability under evidence.

### Narrator

“Professional identity is what you do when pressure makes misconduct convenient.”

### Obserra course controls

- Original question IDs.
- Originality attestation.
- Source mapping.
- Reviewer identity.
- AI tutor lock during assessment.
- No recalled-content ingestion.
- Audit log.

### Tutor speech bubble

“I can generate original ungraded scenarios. I cannot validate an exam dump or provide protected-assessment answers.”

# Sequence 8 — Build the Application Evidence Workbook

## Timecode 22:10–25:10

### Picture

The learner opens the course workbook. A cinematic table assembles:

- Education.
- Project timeline.
- Non-overlap calculation.
- Project objective.
- Leadership responsibility.
- Outcome and value.
- Training ledger.
- Evidence retained.
- Questions for PMI.

### Narrator

“Build your record now, before an application deadline creates pressure.”

### Worked example

#### Project

Enterprise identity-governance implementation.

#### Weak description

“Managed cybersecurity and identity.”

#### Stronger evidence structure

- Temporary initiative: implement enterprise identity-governance capability.
- Objective: reduce excessive access, improve access delivery, and establish auditable lifecycle control.
- Leadership: coordinated business, security, HR, audit, operations, and vendor work.
- Delivery: phased implementation and transition.
- Outcome: operational capability transferred with measures and ownership.

### Narrator

“Do not copy this language unless it is true of your experience. Use the structure, not the claim.”

### Learner prompt

“Describe one qualifying project in 150 to 300 words. Then identify one sentence that is fact, one that is interpretation, and one that needs supporting evidence.”
