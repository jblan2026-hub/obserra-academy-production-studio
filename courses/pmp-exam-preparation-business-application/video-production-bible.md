# PMP Exam Preparation and Business Application

## Cinematic Video Production Bible

**Property of Obserra. Obserra proprietary information. Not for distribution.**

## Production objective

The PMP course must feel like a premium documentary series and an executive simulation environment, not a narrated slide deck. Cinematic quality must serve learning. Every sequence should make a project decision visible: people in conflict, information arriving late, schedules changing, governance paths activating, costs moving, risk becoming an issue, value being measured, and leaders deciding what to do next.

The course is an independent Obserra Academy preparation program aligned to the public July 2026 PMP Examination Content Outline. It is not official PMI courseware, is not sponsored or endorsed by PMI, and does not reproduce confidential examination content. Every factual claim must map to `authoritative-source-register.json`.

## Master delivery specification

- Master resolution: 3840 x 2160 UHD, 16:9.
- Narrative frame rate: 23.976 or 24 fps.
- Screen, diagram, and high-motion inserts: capture at the rate needed for legibility, then conform to the master.
- Color: Rec.709 delivery with high-contrast dark navy, black, gold, restrained holographic blue, and accessible status colors.
- Audio: 48 kHz, dialogue-first mix, online-training target near -16 LUFS integrated, true peak at or below -1 dBTP.
- Captions: human-reviewed WebVTT and SRT.
- Transcript: downloadable, source-linked, and synchronized to chapter markers.
- Audio description: required for essential visual-only information.
- Reduced-motion alternative: required for animated schedules, network maps, and data transitions.
- Source-card duration: long enough for a learner to read owner, title, date, and source identifier.
- All music, footage, photographs, fonts, icons, logos, and likenesses require a rights record.
- AI-generated imagery is permitted only as an illustrative reenactment and must be labeled as such.
- Real public cases must never use synthetic imagery in a way that falsely implies documentary footage.

## Learner experience architecture

Each 60-minute instructional module contains a combination of video, interaction, practice, reflection, and tutor support. A normal module target is:

- 28 to 34 minutes of produced video.
- 8 to 12 minutes of embedded decision interactions.
- 8 to 12 minutes of original practice questions and rationale review.
- 5 to 8 minutes of workbook application.
- Remaining time for AI-supported retrieval practice or reflection.

The protected practice examination sections remain AI-locked until submission.

## Narrative grammar

Every instructional video follows this sequence unless a module-specific reason is documented:

1. **Cold open.** A consequential project decision appears before terminology is introduced.
2. **Decision question.** The learner is asked what the project leader should do first or next.
3. **Why it matters.** Connect the issue to value, people, governance, risk, cost, schedule, compliance, or operations.
4. **Authoritative basis.** Explain the applicable ECO task and supporting project-management guidance.
5. **Facts and data.** Present verified quantitative evidence with source cards and dates.
6. **Documented public case.** Analyze a government, enterprise, or public project record without oversimplifying causation.
7. **Method.** Teach the repeatable decision process and the artifacts it produces.
8. **Business application.** Work an Obserra original scenario from evidence to decision and verification.
9. **Exam reasoning.** Compare the strongest response with plausible but weaker distractors.
10. **Summary and limitations.** State what the learner should know, what remains context-dependent, and what to verify.
11. **Tutor handoff.** Invite ungraded practice and business translation through the course AI tutor.
12. **Knowledge check.** Move into protected or unprotected assessment mode as appropriate.

## Factual density standard

Every module must contain, at minimum:

- Three verified facts or data points where quantitative evidence is appropriate.
- Two source mappings.
- One documented public case or official lessons-learned record.
- One original Obserra business scenario.
- One practical project artifact, calculation, or decision tool.
- One explicit limitations statement.
- One examination reasoning segment.
- One application recommendation for the learner's workplace.

## Source visualization

On-screen source cards use this format:

- Source owner.
- Document title.
- Publication year or date.
- Course source ID.
- `Official source`, `Government audit`, `Professional standard`, `Public case`, or `Instructor interpretation` label.

The video must distinguish controlling requirements, professional guidance, recommended practice, and instructor analysis. These labels are not interchangeable.

## Recurring visual systems

### The project command table

A cinematic table visualization places the current evidence in front of the learner:

- Objective and value.
- Stakeholders and decision rights.
- Delivery approach.
- Scope or backlog.
- Schedule and dependencies.
- Cost and reserves.
- Risk and issues.
- Quality and compliance.
- Change requests.
- Benefits and operational transition.

The table changes as new evidence arrives, demonstrating that good project management is a continuous decision system rather than a static plan.

### The decision lens

Every exam scenario is analyzed through six visible prompts:

1. What environment and delivery approach are present?
2. What fact is most important?
3. What authority does the project manager have?
4. What should happen first or next?
5. Which response protects value, people, evidence, and governance?
6. How will the outcome be verified?

### The distractor wall

Plausible wrong answers appear as transparent panels. Each is rejected for a specific reason:

- Acts before understanding the problem.
- Bypasses the team, stakeholder, product owner, sponsor, or governance body.
- Escalates too early or too late.
- Treats a risk as an issue or an issue as a risk.
- Changes scope without authorization.
- Uses a predictive response in an adaptive context or vice versa.
- Confuses activity with value.
- Ignores ethics, compliance, or evidence.
- Solves the symptom rather than the root cause.

## Module 1 production outline

### Title

Course Orientation, PMP Eligibility, Ethics, and Independent Preparation

### Cold open

A candidate receives an application-audit notice after submitting overlapping project dates and an unsupported claim that a short practice-exam package provided 35 hours of training. The screen freezes before any outcome is shown.

Narrator question: **What evidence should have existed before the application was submitted?**

### Act 1 - What PMI currently requires

Show the four published education-and-experience paths as separate doors. Use a ten-year timeline and visually prevent overlapping months from being counted twice. Explain that the controlling source is the current PMI certification program, not a trainer's marketing claim.

On-screen facts:

- Project experience must fall within the published ten-year window. `[PMI-ECO-2026]`
- The current paths require 60, 48, 36, or 24 months of non-overlapping project leadership experience, depending on education. `[PMI-ECO-2026]`
- Candidates ordinarily need 35 hours of project-management training aligned to the ECO unless an applicable prior-learning path such as an active CAPM credit applies. `[PMI-ECO-2026]`

### Act 2 - What counts as training

Animate a ledger of instructional minutes, objectives, completed lessons, assessment records, source versions, and learner identity. Explain that books and practice exams alone do not satisfy the published training requirement and that PMI may audit and evaluate training quality.

Business artifact: `Training Hour Evidence Ledger`.

### Act 3 - Ethics and exam integrity

Present responsibility, respect, fairness, and honesty as four illuminated pillars. Use an original scenario in which a study-group participant offers recalled exam questions. The learner must reject the material, preserve assessment integrity, and continue with original practice.

### Act 4 - Independent-provider boundary

Display the Obserra and PMI identities separately with a visible independence line:

- Obserra Academy is the course provider.
- PMI owns the certification program and PMP marks.
- This course is independent, not PMI-authorized courseware, and cannot guarantee application acceptance or exam success.

### Act 5 - Readiness baseline

The learner completes an ungraded diagnostic across People, Process, Business Environment, delivery approaches, calculations, ethics, and timing. The AI tutor receives only the submitted results and approved learner context.

### Closing

The candidate from the cold open rebuilds the application record using non-overlapping experience, verified education, and an auditable training ledger. The lesson closes without claiming that PMI will accept a future application; the learner is directed to verify current requirements.

## Module 2 production outline

### Title

2026 Exam Architecture and Scenario-Based Reasoning

### Cold open

A 240-minute clock begins while a complex case study displays stakeholder messages, a burndown chart, a schedule variance, a contract clause, and a new compliance requirement. The learner sees that the challenge is interpretation, not memorization.

### Required data sequence

- 180 total questions.
- 170 scored and 10 pretest questions.
- 240 minutes.
- Two scheduled ten-minute breaks.
- People 33 percent, Process 41 percent, Business Environment 26 percent.
- Approximately 40 percent predictive and 60 percent adaptive or agile and hybrid approaches.
- Question formats include single response, multiple response, case or scenario, graphic-based, matching, drag-and-drop, point-and-click, and pull-down formats, depending on delivery modality.

All claims map to `[PMI-ECO-2026]`.

### Time-management simulation

Learners practice three pacing profiles and see the risk of spending excessive time on one uncertain item. The course does not tell learners to violate test-center rules or use unapproved aids.

### Scenario reasoning sequence

The narrator works four original questions:

1. Conflict in a cross-functional agile team.
2. A predictive schedule baseline threatened by an unresolved dependency.
3. A compliance change requiring governance review.
4. A project delivering outputs while benefits measures show declining value.

Each question is analyzed through the decision lens and distractor wall.

## Public-case production treatment

Documented cases may use public documents, maps, timelines, diagrams, and original visualizations. They may not use misleading reenactments. Every case segment must include:

- What the official source says.
- What the source does not establish.
- Contributing factors rather than a simplistic single cause.
- The relevant PMP task or decision principle.
- A transfer question asking how the learner would apply the lesson elsewhere.

Initial case library:

1. FBI Virtual Case File and Sentinel planning - requirements, governance, procurement, management continuity, and oversight. `[DOJ-OIG-VCF-SENTINEL]`
2. NASA major projects - technical uncertainty, integration and test, reserves, cost, schedule, and portfolio risk. `[GAO-NASA-2018]`
3. James Webb Space Telescope Joint Confidence Level lesson - risk-informed cost and schedule analysis, documentation, and review capability. `[NASA-JWST-JCL]`
4. GSA unforeseen construction conditions - risk identification, incomplete information, and cost or schedule effects. `[GAO-GSA-UNFORESEEN-CONDITIONS]`
5. UK Benefits Payment Card cancellation - commercial structure, dependencies, risk, governance, and major-program lessons. `[NAO-BENEFITS-PAYMENT-CARD]`

## Tutor integration

At the end of every ungraded module, the learner may open the Obserrian PMP Coach to:

- Ask for a simpler or more advanced explanation.
- Request another original example.
- Practice an original scenario.
- Apply the concept to a nonrestricted workplace context.
- Review the source mapping.
- Create a spaced-practice plan.

The tutor is disabled during graded and protected examination sections.
