## Course purpose

The course prepares experienced project leaders to do two things at the same time:

1. Think and decide in the manner required by the July 2026 PMP Examination Content Outline.
2. Apply those same principles to real organizational projects, where information is incomplete, authority is distributed, stakeholders disagree, delivery approaches vary, and success is measured by value and outcomes rather than document completion alone.

## The 2026 examination facts learners must know

The current official outline establishes the following facts. The instructor must present the source date and source identifier when teaching them.

- The examination has 180 questions. Ten are pretest questions and do not count toward the score; candidates cannot identify which items are pretest. `[PMI-ECO-2026]`
- The allotted examination time is 240 minutes. `[PMI-ECO-2026]`
- The exam includes two scheduled ten-minute breaks. A candidate cannot return to the prior section after ending review and starting a break. `[PMI-ECO-2026]`
- The domain distribution is People 33 percent, Process 41 percent, and Business Environment 26 percent. `[PMI-ECO-2026]`
- Approximately 40 percent of items represent predictive approaches; the remaining approximately 60 percent are divided between adaptive or agile and hybrid approaches. These approaches appear across all domains. `[PMI-ECO-2026]`
- The examination may use case or scenario questions, graphic-based questions, multiple-choice, multiple-response, matching, enhanced matching, point-and-click, drag-and-drop, and pull-down formats depending on the delivery modality. `[PMI-ECO-2026]`
- Current eligibility paths use education plus 60, 48, 36, or 24 months of non-overlapping project leadership experience within the preceding ten years. `[PMI-ECO-2026]`
- Candidates generally need at least 35 hours of commercial project-management training aligned to the ECO, unless an applicable prior-learning path such as an active CAPM credit applies. `[PMI-ECO-2026]`
- Books and practice exams alone are not accepted as the 35 hours of training. `[PMI-ECO-2026]`
- PMI may audit training and may deem poor-quality training ineligible. `[PMI-ECO-2026]`
- PMI states that its Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct applies to members, volunteers, certification holders, and certification applicants. `[PMI-ETHICS-2025]`

The instructor must not simplify these facts into a promise that this course will automatically be accepted. The learner must verify current PMI requirements before applying.

## The Obserra decision lens

Every scenario is analyzed through the same six questions:

1. **Environment:** Is the work predictive, adaptive or agile, hybrid, or insufficiently defined?
2. **Evidence:** What fact is most important, and what remains an assumption?
3. **Authority:** Who has the authority to decide, approve, prioritize, accept, or escalate?
4. **Sequence:** What should the project manager do first or next?
5. **Value and protection:** Which response best protects people, value, quality, compliance, evidence, and governance?
6. **Verification:** How will the project manager know the action worked?

Most weak answers fail because they act before diagnosing, bypass the appropriate person or process, escalate prematurely, ignore the delivery approach, accept scope without authority, confuse a risk with an issue, or treat an activity as proof of value.

# Module 1 — Course Orientation, PMP Eligibility, Ethics, and Independent Preparation

## Learning objective

The learner can explain the current eligibility paths, create an auditable training and experience record, protect examination integrity, distinguish an independent course from official PMI courseware, and define a realistic study plan.

## Authoritative basis

Primary sources: `[PMI-ECO-2026]`, `[PMI-PMP-CERTIFICATION]`, `[PMI-PMP-NEW-EXAM-2026]`, and `[PMI-ETHICS-2025]`.

## Instruction

A PMP application is an evidence statement. It is not a marketing biography and it is not a place to inflate titles, overlap months, or describe routine operations as projects. A learner should be able to show what temporary initiative existed, what unique value or outcome it pursued, what leadership responsibility the applicant held, when the work occurred, and how the experience fits the current eligibility path.

The current outline counts non-overlapping months. If two projects ran from February through April, those calendar months cannot be counted twice. The instructor should show a timeline with overlapping projects and ask the learner to calculate eligible time. The point is not arithmetic alone. The point is honesty, auditability, and an accurate statement of professional experience.

The 35-hour requirement is also an evidence obligation. A defensible record identifies the learner, course, provider, learning objectives, instructional duration, completion date, assessment, and course alignment. The learner should retain the completion record, syllabus, and any supporting documentation. The course must record actual completed instructional time rather than assume that enrollment proves completion.

The instructor must explain the planned late-Q4 2026 change described by PMI: live training eligibility is expected to become more restricted, while self-paced courses may be taken from any organization. The exact effective date must be checked at the time of instruction. This course is designed as self-paced on-demand learning. It must not be represented as live ATP training.

## Ethics and examination integrity

The updated PMI ethics resources emphasize responsibility, respect, fairness, and honesty. In exam preparation, that means rejecting recalled questions, exam dumps, unauthorized test banks, or claims that a trainer knows the live exam. The learner should understand that memorizing leaked content weakens professional competence and threatens the credential's integrity.

### Original scenario

A colleague offers a file labeled “July 2026 real PMP questions.” The colleague says everyone in the group is using it and that the questions were reconstructed from memory.

The strongest response is to decline the file, avoid distributing it, use authorized or original practice materials, and follow applicable reporting guidance. The learner should not open the file merely to “verify” whether it is real. The decision protects exam integrity and avoids participating in misconduct.

### Distractor analysis

- **Use the file but do not share it:** still relies on potentially unauthorized content.
- **Compare it with official materials first:** still requires accessing questionable material.
- **Ignore the issue and continue studying:** avoids participation but fails to address possible misconduct where reporting is appropriate.
- **Decline and use original practice:** strongest because it preserves integrity and does not depend on compromised content.

## Business application

Ethical accuracy in an application mirrors ethical accuracy in project reporting. A project manager who misstates experience may also be tempted to hide schedule variance, minimize risk, or overstate benefits. The instructor should connect certification integrity to status reporting, procurement, quality, and governance.

## Practical artifact

Learners complete a **PMP Application Evidence Workbook** with:

- Education path.
- Project dates.
- Non-overlapping month calculation.
- Project objective and unique context.
- Leadership responsibilities.
- Outcome and value.
- Training-hour evidence.
- Supporting documents.
- Assumptions and items requiring PMI confirmation.

## Facts and limitations

The course can explain the public eligibility paths but cannot determine whether PMI will accept a particular credential or experience record. PMI controls the application and audit process.

# Module 2 — 2026 Exam Architecture and Scenario-Based Reasoning

## Learning objective

The learner can explain the exam structure, use a pacing plan, interpret new case and graphic-based formats, and apply the decision lens to select the best response rather than a merely possible response.

## Authoritative basis

Primary sources: `[PMI-ECO-2026]` and `[PMI-PMP-NEW-EXAM-2026]`.

## Instruction

The July 2026 exam is an application examination. The outline explicitly distinguishes the examination from any single reference book. A candidate must use project-management concepts and experience to reason through work situations. That makes context, sequence, authority, and delivery approach critical.

A 240-minute exam with 180 questions provides an average of 80 seconds per item. That average is not a rigid rule because case sections and graphic questions require more reading, while some independent items require less. The learner should practice pacing by section and use confidence markers rather than spending excessive time forcing certainty on one question.

The two scheduled breaks create decision gates. Once a candidate starts a break after reviewing the previous section, the candidate cannot return to that section. The learner must build a section-review strategy before exam day.

## Domain and approach mix

The instructor should use a 100-unit visual model:

- People: 33 units.
- Process: 41 units.
- Business Environment: 26 units.

Then overlay the delivery-approach mix:

- Approximately 40 units predictive.
- Approximately 60 units adaptive or agile and hybrid.

The key lesson is that an agile question is not confined to People and a predictive question is not confined to Process. Governance, compliance, risk, stakeholders, quality, and value appear in every environment.

## Question-format preparation

### Case or scenario

The learner receives a detailed situation and may answer several questions from the same evidence set. Teach the learner to separate stable facts from question-specific facts and to avoid importing assumptions.

### Graphic-based

The learner interprets charts, schedules, flow diagrams, risk data, or financial information. Teach the learner to identify the chart's unit, baseline, date, source, and limitation before answering.

### Multiple-response

The learner must select more than one correct response. Teach the learner to evaluate each option independently rather than searching for one “best” answer.

### Matching and point-and-click

Teach relationships, sequencing, and visual location. Practice must be accessible and include keyboard alternatives.

## Original example 1 — Conflict in an agile team

A senior architect and product owner disagree over whether a security control should delay a release. The team has stopped discussing the issue and is waiting for the project manager to decide.

The strongest first action is to facilitate analysis of the conflict, surface the underlying interests and constraints, and help the appropriate roles reach an evidence-based decision. Immediately overruling the product owner or architect ignores the team and the decision context. Escalating to the sponsor before attempting resolution may be premature unless authority or safety thresholds require it.

## Original example 2 — Predictive schedule dependency

A critical vendor deliverable is late. The schedule shows that the activity is on the critical path and has no remaining float. The vendor promises recovery but provides no evidence.

The project manager should analyze the schedule impact, validate the vendor's recovery plan, update risk and issue records, and use the contract and governance paths. Simply adding resources without understanding the dependency may not help. Hiding the delay until the next status cycle weakens governance.

## Original example 3 — New compliance requirement

A regulator issues a requirement that affects data retention in a product under development. The team proposes implementing a technical change immediately.

The strongest response begins by confirming applicability, analyzing impact, and using the appropriate change and compliance processes. Immediate implementation may create new risk if the requirement is misunderstood or the change bypasses approval and validation.

## Original example 4 — Output without value

A project has delivered every planned feature, but adoption is low and the measured business benefit is declining.

The project manager should bring outcome evidence to the sponsor and stakeholders, reassess assumptions and value measures, and recommend an appropriate response. Declaring success because scope is complete confuses output with value.

## Practical artifact

Learners create:

- Personal pacing plan.
- Section review checklist.
- Question-type performance baseline.
- Confidence calibration log.
- Decision-lens worksheet.

## Limitation

No pacing strategy guarantees a passing result. Candidates must adapt to their reading speed, accommodations, delivery modality, and exam experience.

# People Domain Modules

## Module 3 — Develop and Sustain a Common Vision

### Core instruction

A common vision explains why the project exists, what future state it intends to create, which outcomes matter, and how stakeholders will recognize value. A project manager keeps the vision current when evidence, strategy, regulation, or market conditions change. The project manager does not treat a charter statement as permanently sufficient.

### Facts and evidence

The 2026 ECO explicitly includes creating, promoting, refreshing, and diagnosing misunderstanding of the vision. `[PMI-ECO-2026]`

### Business application

For an enterprise identity-modernization project, the weak vision is “deploy a new identity platform.” The stronger vision is “reduce unauthorized access and access-delivery time while preserving workforce productivity and auditable accountability.” The stronger version allows the team to compare features with outcomes.

### Exam pattern

When stakeholders disagree about purpose, clarify and align the vision before optimizing detailed execution. Do not add scope simply to satisfy the loudest stakeholder.

### Artifact

Vision-to-value map with outcomes, measures, stakeholder value, assumptions, and review triggers.

## Module 4 — Manage Conflict and Ground Rules

### Core instruction

Conflict is information. The project manager identifies the source, context, urgency, relationship, power dynamics, and impact before choosing a strategy. Collaboration is often strong when time and trust permit, but no single technique is always correct. Safety, ethics, or urgent operational harm may require immediate intervention.

### Original scenario

Two engineering leads disagree about whether to refactor a component before a product launch. One frames the issue as quality; the other frames it as schedule. The project manager facilitates evidence review: defect trends, technical-debt risk, release value, recovery options, and acceptance criteria.

### Exam pattern

Prefer direct, respectful resolution at the lowest appropriate level. Escalate when authority, policy, unresolved impact, or safety requires it. Do not avoid conflict and hope it disappears.

### Artifact

Conflict diagnosis and resolution record.

## Module 5 — Lead and Empower the Project Team

### Core instruction

Leadership is situational. The project manager clarifies outcomes and boundaries, then gives the team authority appropriate to competence, risk, and governance. Empowerment is not absence of accountability. It includes clear decision rights, access to information, escalation paths, and support.

### Business example

A distributed cybersecurity team has specialists in cloud, identity, and incident response. A command-and-control approach makes every decision wait for the project manager. A better model establishes architecture and security guardrails, assigns domain ownership, and reserves cross-domain decisions for the integrated governance forum.

### Exam pattern

Coach and enable before replacing a capable team member. Address systemic barriers before blaming individuals. Use servant leadership in adaptive contexts without abandoning governance.

### Artifact

Team charter and decision-rights matrix.

## Module 6 — Engage Stakeholders

### Core instruction

Stakeholder analysis is dynamic. Influence, interest, impact, support, and information needs can change. Engagement is not the same as broadcasting status. It includes listening, negotiation, trust, involvement, and feedback.

### Original scenario

A clinical product project has users, patients, security, privacy, legal, regulators, operations, finance, and vendors. A generic weekly email does not engage them. The project manager creates role-specific decision forums and feedback channels.

### Exam pattern

Identify and analyze before selecting communication. Engage affected people early, especially when their knowledge can change requirements or adoption.

### Artifact

Stakeholder register, engagement assessment, and tailored plan.

## Module 7 — Align Stakeholder Expectations

### Core instruction

Alignment is not unanimous agreement. It is a transparent understanding of objectives, tradeoffs, constraints, decision rights, and what success means. The project manager surfaces contradictions rather than documenting incompatible promises.

### Exam pattern

Facilitate alignment before accepting competing expectations into scope. Use sponsor or governance authority when tradeoffs exceed the team’s authority.

### Artifact

Expectation matrix and alignment workshop record.

## Module 8 — Manage Customer and Stakeholder Expectations

### Core instruction

Expectation management continues throughout delivery. The project manager monitors satisfaction, validates outcomes, communicates uncertainty, and distinguishes an approved change from informal pressure.

### Original scenario

A customer asks the team to “just add” an analytics feature because the release is still two months away. The project manager explores value and urgency, assesses impact, and routes the request through the product or change process. Immediate acceptance is not customer service; it is uncontrolled commitment.

### Artifact

Expectation baseline, feedback loop, and change decision.

## Module 9 — Ensure Knowledge Transfer

### Core instruction

Knowledge transfer begins before closure. Critical knowledge includes decisions, assumptions, operating procedures, design rationale, vendor dependencies, failure modes, and support contacts. Documentation alone is not proof of transfer; the receiving team must demonstrate readiness.

### Exam pattern

Plan transfer with the people who will use the knowledge. Use demonstrations, pairing, rehearsal, and acceptance criteria rather than a final document dump.

### Artifact

Knowledge inventory and operational handoff package.

## Module 10 — Plan Communication, Reporting, and Feedback

### Core instruction

Communication plans define purpose, audience, decision, content, frequency, channel, owner, confidentiality, and feedback. A status report should help someone decide, not merely prove activity.

### Business application

An executive report may need value, risk, forecast, decisions, and confidence. A team report may need blockers, dependencies, quality, and next work. A regulator may need evidence and traceability. One report cannot serve every purpose.

### Artifact

Communication strategy and governance-reporting matrix.

# Process Domain Modules

## Module 11 — Develop the Integrated Plan and Delivery Approach

### Core instruction

The delivery approach is selected from context, not ideology. Predictive approaches work well when scope and solution are sufficiently stable and control requires baselines. Adaptive approaches help when uncertainty is high and learning can be delivered incrementally. Hybrid approaches combine methods intentionally; they are not an excuse for inconsistent governance.

### Documented case

The DOJ OIG reported that the FBI's terminated Virtual Case File effort suffered from poorly defined and evolving requirements, investment-management weaknesses, management discontinuity, and weak oversight. `[DOJ-OIG-VCF-SENTINEL]` The lesson is not that every large system must be agile. The lesson is that requirements, governance, integration, procurement, and leadership continuity must fit the work.

### Artifact

Delivery-approach assessment and integrated plan.

## Module 12 — Develop and Manage Scope

### Core instruction

Scope describes boundaries. In predictive work, a scope statement and work breakdown structure help establish and control the baseline. In adaptive work, the product goal, roadmap, backlog, acceptance criteria, and definition of done provide evolving control. Both require transparency and authority.

### Exam pattern

Clarify requirements with stakeholders and the team before rejecting or accepting change. Do not secretly add work to protect a relationship.

### Artifact

Scope statement, backlog, WBS, and acceptance criteria.

## Module 13 — Ensure Value-Based Delivery

### Core instruction

Value is the benefit worth the effort and expense. Outputs are necessary but may not produce outcomes. The project manager works with sponsors, product leaders, customers, and benefits owners to define measures, prioritize value, and reassess assumptions.

### Example

A platform migration completes on time but user productivity declines. Technical completion is not sufficient evidence of value. The team must analyze adoption, process design, training, performance, and benefits.

### Artifact

Benefits register and outcome-measurement plan.

## Module 14 — Plan and Manage Resources

### Core instruction

Resource planning includes people, skills, equipment, facilities, funding, data, environments, and supplier capacity. A named person is not automatically available or qualified. The project manager analyzes constraints and negotiates tradeoffs.

### Exam pattern

Do not solve every shortage by demanding overtime. Analyze priorities, capacity, skills, dependencies, and alternatives.

### Artifact

Capacity plan and resource calendar.

## Module 15 — Plan and Manage Procurement

### Core instruction

Contract type allocates risk. Fixed-price arrangements may transfer some cost risk but can increase change friction when scope is uncertain. Cost-reimbursable structures may support uncertainty but require strong oversight. Time-and-materials arrangements need ceilings, monitoring, and clear outcomes.

### Documented case

The Virtual Case File and Benefits Payment Card cases show that weak requirements, commercial structure, integration, and oversight can interact. `[DOJ-OIG-VCF-SENTINEL]` `[NAO-BENEFITS-PAYMENT-CARD]`

### Artifact

Make-or-buy analysis, contract-risk matrix, and vendor scorecard.

## Module 16 — Plan and Manage Project Finance

### Core instruction

Teach cost baseline, contingency reserve, management reserve, forecast, and variance. Learners practice earned-value concepts as decision tools rather than formula memorization.

### Core formulas

- Cost variance: `CV = EV - AC`.
- Schedule variance: `SV = EV - PV`.
- Cost performance index: `CPI = EV / AC`.
- Schedule performance index: `SPI = EV / PV`.
- A common estimate at completion when current cost efficiency is expected to continue: `EAC = BAC / CPI`.
- Estimate to complete: `ETC = EAC - AC`.

The instructor must explain that formula choice depends on assumptions. A calculated number is not automatically a reliable forecast.

### Documented case

GAO found cost and schedule deterioration across NASA major projects and linked some problems to aggressive schedules, reserve levels, technical issues, and integration-and-test challenges. `[GAO-NASA-2018]`

### Artifact

Cost baseline, reserve analysis, and forecast narrative.

## Module 17 — Optimize Quality, Compliance, and Sustainability

### Core instruction

Quality is planned, built, evaluated, and improved. It is not a final test. Teach prevention, appraisal, failure cost, acceptance criteria, quality metrics, control limits, root-cause analysis, and continuous improvement.

Compliance requirements may include law, regulation, contract, policy, security, health and safety, privacy, and sustainability. The project manager identifies applicability with qualified stakeholders and does not make unsupported legal conclusions.

### Exam pattern

When a defect trend emerges, analyze and improve the process rather than relying only on more inspection.

### Artifact

Quality plan, compliance evidence map, and cost-of-quality analysis.

## Module 18 — Plan and Manage the Schedule

### Core instruction

A reliable schedule includes all work, logical sequencing, realistic durations, resources, calendars, dependencies, critical path, float, baseline, and status discipline. The critical path is the longest logical path through the network under the model's assumptions.

### Facts and data

GAO's Schedule Assessment Guide describes ten best practices for reliable schedules and emphasizes that an integrated schedule supports realism, change analysis, and management warning. `[GAO-SCHEDULE-GUIDE]`

NASA's lessons-learned record on the James Webb Space Telescope describes risk-informed cost and schedule analysis using a network schedule, resource loading, risk-register inputs, and Monte Carlo simulation. `[NASA-JWST-JCL]`

### Artifact

Network diagram, critical-path analysis, baseline, and schedule-risk narrative.

## Module 19 — Evaluate Status, Metrics, and Artifacts

### Core instruction

Status is a reconciliation of plan, actuals, forecast, risk, value, and decisions. Metrics need definition, source, owner, frequency, threshold, and limitation. Artifacts should be tailored, current, accessible, and useful.

### Exam pattern

If a dashboard says “green” but source evidence shows declining value or hidden variance, investigate and correct the representation. Do not optimize presentation while evidence is weak.

### Artifact

Metric dictionary and decision dashboard.

## Module 20 — Close the Project or Phase and Transition Outcomes

### Core instruction

Closure confirms acceptance, transition readiness, contract and financial completion, resource release, records, lessons, and benefits ownership. In adaptive delivery, releases may be frequent, but product or project closure still requires governance and operational accountability.

### Exam pattern

Do not close solely because the team finished work. Validate acceptance and transition readiness with the appropriate stakeholders.

### Artifact

Closure checklist and operational-readiness assessment.

# Business Environment Modules

## Module 21 — Define and Establish Project Governance

### Core instruction

Governance establishes decision rights, forums, thresholds, policies, reporting, ethics, and accountability. The project manager tailors governance to complexity, risk, regulation, and organizational context.

### Documented case

DOJ OIG reporting on the FBI technology program identified continuity and oversight weaknesses. `[DOJ-OIG-VCF-SENTINEL]` The lesson is that governance must operate through capable people, evidence, and decisions; a diagram alone is not governance.

### Artifact

Governance charter and escalation matrix.

## Module 22 — Plan and Manage Compliance

### Core instruction

Start with applicability. Classify obligations as law, regulation, contract, policy, standard, or guidance. Engage legal, compliance, security, safety, privacy, quality, or other qualified owners. Map each obligation to controls, evidence, owner, timing, and response.

### Exam pattern

Do not wait for final inspection. Integrate compliance into requirements, design, backlog, quality, risk, and change control.

### Artifact

Obligation register and compliance evidence plan.

## Module 23 — Manage and Control Changes

### Core instruction

Change control is not synonymous with bureaucracy. It ensures that impact, authority, value, and records are considered. Predictive projects may use a formal change-control board. Adaptive products may reprioritize a backlog within delegated authority. Hybrid environments need explicit boundaries.

### Exam pattern

Understand the approach and decision rights before choosing the change path.

### Artifact

Change request, impact analysis, decision, and updated baseline or backlog.

## Module 24 — Remove Impediments and Manage Issues

### Core instruction

A risk is an uncertain event or condition; an issue has occurred or is occurring. An impediment obstructs progress. The project manager assesses impact, prioritizes, intervenes, collaborates, and verifies removal.

### Exam pattern

Do not merely log an urgent issue. Act within authority and escalate when thresholds require it.

### Artifact

Impediment log, issue record, and verification evidence.

## Module 25 — Plan and Manage Risk

### Core instruction

Risk management includes threats and opportunities. Teach identification, qualitative analysis, quantitative analysis when justified, response ownership, monitoring, triggers, residual risk, secondary risk, and communication.

### Facts and data

GAO reviewed 18 GSA repair and alteration projects; 11 experienced unforeseen conditions. In nine of those 11, remediation cost was 1 to 5 percent of the original construction award, and one was about 6 percent. `[GAO-GSA-UNFORESEEN-CONDITIONS]` This is a case-specific sample, not a universal benchmark.

### Quantitative tools

- Expected monetary value: probability multiplied by impact.
- Decision trees for alternatives under uncertainty.
- Three-point estimation and simulation where assumptions justify them.

### Artifact

Risk register, analysis, response plan, and monitoring record.

## Module 26 — Drive Continuous Improvement

### Core instruction

A retrospective without action is a meeting, not improvement. Improvement requires a change, owner, due date, measure, and follow-up. Update organizational process assets only after review and approval.

### Artifact

Improvement backlog and effectiveness measure.

## Module 27 — Support Organizational Change

### Core instruction

A technically successful project can fail in adoption. Assess culture, incentives, sponsorship, readiness, communication, training, process change, resistance, and reinforcement. Project and change leadership must be coordinated.

### Exam pattern

Engage affected stakeholders early and address root causes of resistance rather than labeling people as the problem.

### Artifact

Change-impact and adoption plan.

## Module 28 — Evaluate External Environment Changes

### Core instruction

Monitor regulatory, technological, geopolitical, market, supply-chain, environmental, and competitive signals. Assess relevance and impact before changing scope or backlog.

### Example

A new privacy requirement affects an AI product. The project manager confirms applicability with qualified owners, analyzes architecture and data-flow impacts, updates risk and compliance evidence, and uses the authorized change path.

### Artifact

External-environment scan and governance briefing.

# Integrated Practice Modules

## Module 29 — Predictive, Adaptive, and Hybrid Tailoring

The learner compares three delivery models for the same enterprise transformation. The instructor changes uncertainty, regulation, dependency, supplier, and release conditions and asks learners to tailor governance, planning, metrics, and controls.

A predictive approach is not inherently rigid. An adaptive approach is not inherently uncontrolled. Hybrid is not a random mixture. Strong tailoring explains why each element exists and how the system remains coherent.

## Module 30 — AI, Sustainability, Stakeholder Value, and Business Impact

AI may support estimation, risk identification, summarization, scenario analysis, communication, and knowledge retrieval. Human leaders remain accountable for data quality, privacy, bias, security, source verification, judgment, and authorization.

The July 2026 update explicitly raises AI, sustainability, stakeholder engagement, value, and business impact. `[PMI-PMP-NEW-EXAM-2026]` The course teaches these as integrated project considerations, not isolated buzzwords.

Learners produce an AI-use decision record containing purpose, data classification, tool authority, validation, limitations, human review, and evidence.

## Module 31 — Business Application Capstone and Evidence-Based Readiness

### Capstone scenario

A global organization is replacing a fragmented customer platform. The program includes:

- A predictive infrastructure migration.
- An adaptive customer-experience product.
- A hybrid data-governance workstream.
- A regulated data-retention requirement.
- A fixed-price systems integrator and a time-and-materials specialist vendor.
- A board commitment to measurable customer and operating value.
- AI-assisted requirements and testing tools.
- Workforce resistance and regional privacy concerns.

Learners must create:

1. Vision and value map.
2. Stakeholder strategy.
3. Delivery-approach rationale.
4. Integrated milestone and dependency view.
5. Scope and backlog boundaries.
6. Procurement and contract-risk analysis.
7. Cost, reserve, and forecast summary.
8. Risk and issue register.
9. Quality and compliance evidence map.
10. Change and governance model.
11. Adoption and knowledge-transfer plan.
12. Executive briefing with confidence and limitations.

## Practice Examination Sections 32–34

The three protected sections contain 60 original questions each. The total blueprint must match the ECO domain weights within the limits of integer allocation and approximate the predictive versus adaptive or hybrid mix. Each question includes:

- Domain and task.
- Delivery approach.
- Question type.
- Difficulty.
- Correct response.
- Rationale.
- Distractor analysis.
- Source mapping.
- Originality attestation.

The AI tutor is locked during each section. After submission, the learner receives task-level and question-type analysis.

## Final readiness review

A learner is not told simply, “You are ready.” The readiness decision considers:

- Consistent performance across all domains.
- No critical task blind spots.
- Performance in predictive and adaptive or hybrid scenarios.
- Case and graphic-based question performance.
- Pacing and break strategy.
- Confidence calibration.
- Ethical preparation.
- Ability to explain why answers are correct.

The learner creates a final remediation plan and decides when to schedule the exam based on evidence, personal obligations, and current PMI eligibility.
