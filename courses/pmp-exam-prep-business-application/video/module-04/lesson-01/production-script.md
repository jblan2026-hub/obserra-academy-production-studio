## Scene 3. The exam architecture makes tailoring unavoidable, 03:15 to 05:35

**VISUAL**

A 100-unit field divides into approximately 40 predictive units and 60 adaptive or agile and hybrid units. The units flow through People, Process, and Business Environment.

**NARRATOR**

The public July 2026 PMP Examination Content Outline states that approximately 40 percent of the examination represents predictive approaches. The remaining approximately 60 percent is divided between adaptive or agile and hybrid approaches.

The exam does not treat these approaches as isolated vocabularies. A stakeholder, risk, procurement, governance, or team question may occur in any delivery environment.

**ON SCREEN EXAM RULE**

> Read the evidence of how work is planned, delivered, governed, reviewed, and changed. Do not answer from labels alone.

**EXAMPLES OF MISLEADING LABELS**

1. A team says “agile” but releases annually and requires executive approval for every backlog change.
2. A project says “predictive” but requirements are intentionally discovered through prototypes.
3. A program says “hybrid” but has no integration model between its workstreams.

## Scene 4. The tailoring diagnostic, 05:35 to 10:15

**VISUAL**

A twelve-factor tailoring model forms.

### 1. Product and solution certainty

How well is the desired solution understood?

### 2. Requirements volatility

How likely are needs, priorities, regulations, or constraints to change?

### 3. Value cadence

Can useful value be delivered incrementally, or does value require one integrated completion?

### 4. Technical uncertainty

How much discovery, experimentation, prototyping, or learning is required?

### 5. Safety and consequence

What harm can result from failure, incomplete integration, or uncontrolled change?

### 6. Regulation and assurance

Which evidence, reviews, validation, approvals, or traceability are required?

### 7. Stakeholder availability

Can users and decision makers provide frequent feedback?

### 8. Contract and procurement structure

Does the agreement support evolving scope, fixed deliverables, outcomes, incentives, or shared risk?

### 9. Organizational capability

Do teams, leaders, tools, culture, and governance support the chosen approach?

### 10. Dependencies and integration

How tightly coupled are workstreams, suppliers, systems, facilities, and release milestones?

### 11. Funding and decision cadence

How are budgets, approvals, benefits, and risk decisions made?

### 12. Geography and operating environment

How do time zones, languages, facilities, supply chains, security, and local obligations affect delivery?

**NARRATOR**

Tailoring is the deliberate adjustment of methods, artifacts, roles, governance, and controls to fit the work.

## Scene 5. Predictive delivery evidence, 10:15 to 13:10

**VISUAL**

A predictive project model appears with integrated plans, baselines, dependencies, review gates, and formal acceptance.

**PREDICTIVE INDICATORS**

1. Scope and solution can be defined sufficiently early.
2. Work follows strong dependencies or physical sequence.
3. Changes require integrated impact analysis.
4. Contracts define deliverables and acceptance.
5. Regulatory or safety evidence must follow controlled stages.
6. Forecast and baseline performance are important governance tools.
7. Value is realized primarily after integrated delivery.

**NARRATOR**

Predictive does not mean rigid or unable to learn. It means that planned sequence, baselines, and controlled change provide useful decision value.

**ORIGINAL EXAMPLE**

A utility is replacing a high-voltage substation. Engineering, procurement, permits, site preparation, equipment lead times, installation, testing, and energization have strong dependencies. Formal planning and control are necessary, even if digital monitoring software is developed adaptively.

## Scene 6. Adaptive delivery evidence, 13:10 to 16:00

**VISUAL**

An adaptive product model appears with product goal, prioritized backlog, short feedback cycles, increments, and changing forecasts.

**ADAPTIVE INDICATORS**

1. The problem or solution requires discovery.
2. Users can provide frequent feedback.
3. Value can be delivered in small increments.
4. Priorities may change as evidence changes.
5. Cross-functional teams can make local decisions.
6. Technical architecture supports iterative delivery.
7. Flow, outcomes, and feedback provide more value than a detailed long-range task baseline.

**NARRATOR**

Adaptive delivery does not eliminate planning. It changes the horizon and level of commitment.

Teams may plan product outcomes and release goals broadly while planning near-term work in more detail. Governance should focus on value, risk, evidence, and decision thresholds rather than approving every task.

**ORIGINAL EXAMPLE**

A customer analytics product begins with uncertain user needs and several model options. The team tests small capabilities, validates data quality and usefulness, and reprioritizes based on evidence.

## Scene 7. Hybrid delivery as an integration design, 16:00 to 19:15

**VISUAL**

A hybrid program has three workstreams:

1. Facility and hardware, predictive.
2. Application and user experience, adaptive.
3. Data migration and regulatory validation, staged hybrid.

Shared milestones, interfaces, configuration controls, and governance connect them.

**NARRATOR**

Hybrid delivery is not simply using some meetings from one method and some artifacts from another. It requires a coherent integration model.

A hybrid design should define:

1. Which work uses which approach and why.
2. Shared outcomes and release milestones.
3. Interface and dependency ownership.
4. Configuration and change control.
5. Integrated risk and quality evidence.
6. Funding and decision cadence.
7. Cross-workstream metrics.
8. Escalation and synchronization points.
9. Acceptance and transition criteria.

**ON SCREEN WARNING**

> Multiple approaches without integration create multiple versions of reality.

## Scene 8. Tailoring governance and artifacts together, 19:15 to 22:15

**VISUAL**

A tailoring decision changes several parts of the project system simultaneously.

### Planning

Rolling-wave plan, baseline plan, roadmap, release plan, backlog, milestone network, or a combination.

### Governance

Decision thresholds, review cadence, delegated authority, release approval, risk acceptance, and evidence requirements.

### Artifacts

Charter, roadmap, backlog, integrated schedule, cost model, risk register, decision records, architecture records, and control evidence.

### Roles

Sponsor, project manager, product owner, technical leads, control owners, operations, users, and suppliers.

### Metrics

Forecast accuracy, flow, cycle time, milestone performance, cost, quality, adoption, value, risk, and confidence.

**NARRATOR**

A project cannot safely delegate backlog priority while retaining a monthly approval process for every requirement detail. It cannot remove baselines without defining how commitments and forecasts will be governed. Tailoring must remain internally consistent.

## Scene 9. Guidance-based example: integrated schedule and cost evidence, 22:15 to 24:35

**SOURCE CARD**

U.S. Government Accountability Office  
GAO Schedule Assessment Guide, GAO 16 89G  
GAO Cost Estimating and Assessment Guide, GAO 20 195G

**NARRATOR**

GAO guidance describes reliable schedules and cost estimates as decision tools. An integrated schedule can show whether dates and milestones are realistic and how changes affect the program. Reliable cost estimating supports budgeting, oversight, and performance evaluation.

The lesson is not that every project must use one government template. The lesson is that when schedule and cost commitments matter, the chosen approach still needs credible evidence.

An adaptive team may forecast capacity, release range, and cost using empirical data. A predictive program may use network schedules and baseline cost. A hybrid program may need both, connected through common assumptions and governance.

**LIMITATIONS CARD**

Government guidance should be tailored to context. It does not prescribe one method for every commercial, nonprofit, or international project.

## Scene 10. Original enterprise scenario: Orion Medical Platform, 24:35 to 29:05

**LABEL**

Obserra original synthetic scenario. Not a documented public case.

**SCENARIO**

Orion is developing a connected medical platform.

1. Hardware design and manufacturing have long lead times and formal verification.
2. Software requirements will evolve through clinician feedback.
3. Cybersecurity evidence must be traceable across hardware, software, cloud, and operations.
4. The sales team wants quarterly customer demonstrations.
5. A supplier contract assumes fixed interface dates.
6. The executive committee wants one portfolio forecast.

**QUESTION**

Which delivery approach is strongest?

**OPTIONS**

A. Use a single adaptive method for every workstream and eliminate formal milestones.  
B. Use a fully predictive method and freeze all software requirements before clinician feedback.  
C. Design a governed hybrid approach with predictive hardware and assurance milestones, adaptive software increments, and explicit integration, configuration, risk, evidence, and forecast controls.  
D. Allow every team to choose independently without shared governance.

**NARRATOR**

Option C is strongest. The work has different uncertainty and control needs, but the product must integrate into one safe and supportable system.

**FOLLOW UP**

The project defines:

1. Hardware design and verification gates.
2. Software release goals and short feedback cycles.
3. Shared interface baselines.
4. Cybersecurity evidence requirements.
5. Integrated system demonstrations.
6. Supplier decision points.
7. One risk and executive forecast model.
8. Final operational acceptance and monitoring.

## Scene 11. Reassessing the approach, 29:05 to 31:15

**VISUAL**

A tailoring decision is reviewed when conditions change.

1. Requirements stabilize.
2. Regulation changes.
3. A supplier fails.
4. User access declines.
5. Technical uncertainty increases.
6. Architecture allows smaller releases.
7. The organization gains or loses adaptive capability.

**NARRATOR**

Tailoring is not a one-time declaration. The project manager should review whether the delivery approach still supports value, quality, risk, governance, and stakeholder needs.

Changes to the approach should be analyzed and communicated. A team should not quietly abandon required controls or commitments under the label of adaptation.

## Scene 12. Common PMP tailoring traps, 31:15 to 33:00

### Trap 1. Choose the method preferred by leadership

Assess the work and context first.

### Trap 2. Assume agile means no plan or documentation

Adaptive teams plan and document what is necessary for value, coordination, evidence, and governance.

### Trap 3. Assume predictive means no change

Predictive projects manage change through integrated analysis and authorization.

### Trap 4. Call any mixture hybrid

Hybrid requires explicit integration and shared governance.

### Trap 5. Tailor away compliance or ethics

Tailoring cannot eliminate controlling obligations or human accountability.

### Trap 6. Copy a method from another project

Use prior evidence, but reassess product, stakeholders, risk, capability, and environment.

## Knowledge check package

### Question 1

A product has uncertain user needs and can deliver value incrementally. Which approach is most appropriate to consider?

A. Adaptive delivery with frequent feedback and reprioritization.  
B. A fixed long-range task baseline with no changes.  
C. No planning.  
D. Immediate project closure.

**Correct response:** A.

### Question 2

What is the most important feature of a hybrid approach?

A. Using more than one terminology set.  
B. Explicit integration of workstreams, interfaces, governance, risk, evidence, and milestones.  
C. Allowing each team to ignore common commitments.  
D. Eliminating configuration control.

**Correct response:** B.

### Question 3

A team calls itself agile but every backlog change requires executive approval. What should the project manager do?

A. Accept the label.  
B. Analyze actual decision rights, feedback, planning, and delivery behavior and tailor the operating model.  
C. Remove the backlog.  
D. Add more ceremonies.

**Correct response:** B.

### Question 4

When should the delivery approach be reassessed?

A. Never after the charter.  
B. When evidence, uncertainty, regulation, capability, dependencies, or value cadence materially changes.  
C. Only after project closure.  
D. Whenever one team member requests it, without analysis.

**Correct response:** B.

## Closing summary, 33:00 to 34:10

**NARRATOR**

Select and tailor the delivery approach from evidence about the work. Predictive delivery supports defined commitments and integrated control. Adaptive delivery supports discovery, feedback, and incremental value. Hybrid delivery coordinates different approaches through one coherent project system.
