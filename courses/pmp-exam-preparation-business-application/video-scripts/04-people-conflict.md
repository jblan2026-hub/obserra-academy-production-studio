# Sequence 1: Cold Open, The Release Decision

## Timecode 00:00 to 03:15

### Picture

Black screen. A heartbeat and a distant alarm tone build beneath the gold Obserra ident.

Cut to a cinematic software release command center at 10:47 p.m. A global product launch is scheduled for midnight.

The product owner stands beside a release dashboard.

> Customer demand is high. The revenue commitment is public. Release tonight.

The security architect opens a control finding.

> A privileged service account can perform actions beyond its approved role. Release must stop.

The engineering lead adds another fact.

> The weakness is real, but exploitation requires internal access. A temporary containment option exists.

The compliance lead speaks from a remote screen.

> The control obligation is not yet confirmed. We need the authoritative owner.

The team has stopped collaborating. Each person is repeating a position. The project manager is asked to decide immediately.

### On screen decision question

**What should the project manager do first?**

1. Support the security architect because security always has final authority.
2. Support the product owner because the business commitment has already been announced.
3. Facilitate an evidence based conflict analysis that identifies the issue, interests, decision rights, applicable constraints, and available options.
4. Escalate directly to the chief executive officer without attempting resolution.

The learner selects an answer. The system records it but delays feedback.

### Narrator

“Conflict is not proof that a team has failed. Conflict is information. It can reveal different objectives, incomplete evidence, unclear authority, competing risks, hidden assumptions, or incompatible commitments.”

“The project manager does not choose a side merely because one role sounds more urgent. The project manager first understands what is actually in conflict and who has authority to decide.”

# Sequence 2: What the Examination Is Testing

## Timecode 03:15 to 05:30

### Picture

The release command center freezes. The four positions separate into an original Obserra conflict model.

1. Source of conflict.
2. Context.
3. Resolution strategy.
4. Team ground rules.

### Narrator

“The current examination outline expects the project leader to identify the source and stage of conflict, analyze the context, choose an appropriate resolution strategy, and establish or reinforce team ground rules.”

“Many exam distractors are technically possible but procedurally weak. They avoid the issue, bypass the team, escalate before diagnosis, impose authority the project manager does not possess, or optimize one objective while ignoring another.”

### On screen exam lens

1. What happened?
2. What is each party trying to protect or achieve?
3. Which facts are verified?
4. What assumptions or emotions are influencing the disagreement?
5. What is the urgency and potential consequence?
6. What decision rights and policies apply?
7. Can the conflict be resolved directly at the working level?
8. What action protects people, value, evidence, and governance?
9. How will the agreement be documented and verified?

### Narrator

“The strongest answer depends on context. Collaboration is often effective, but a potential safety, legal, ethical, security, or material business impact may require immediate protective action and involvement of an authorized role.”

# Sequence 3: Diagnose Before Selecting a Technique

## Timecode 05:30 to 09:20

### Picture

A conflict diagnostic engine builds across the screen. Each dimension changes the appropriate response.

### Dimension 1: Conflict subject

1. Goals or priorities.
2. Requirements or scope.
3. Technical method.
4. Resources or schedule.
5. Roles and authority.
6. Quality, safety, security, or compliance.
7. Values, behavior, or ethics.
8. Communication and misunderstanding.

### Dimension 2: Stage

1. Latent concern.
2. Recognized disagreement.
3. Active debate.
4. Behavioral disruption.
5. Work stoppage or decision paralysis.
6. Harm, retaliation, misconduct, or uncontrolled escalation.

### Dimension 3: Context

1. Predictive, adaptive, or hybrid environment.
2. Time pressure.
3. Consequence severity.
4. Power imbalance.
5. Cultural and communication context.
6. History and trust.
7. Policy or contract.
8. Decision authority.
9. Evidence quality.

### Dimension 4: Desired outcome

1. Clarification.
2. Joint problem solving.
3. Negotiated tradeoff.
4. Authoritative decision.
5. Immediate protection.
6. Formal investigation or escalation.
7. Relationship repair.
8. Updated ground rule or governance mechanism.

### Narrator

“A conflict resolution technique is not selected from a memorized preference. It is selected from the nature of the conflict, the people involved, the risk, the evidence, the authority, and the outcome required.”

### Original micro examples

**Technical disagreement:** Two engineers prefer different database architectures. The team can compare requirements, risks, performance evidence, cost, and maintainability.

**Priority disagreement:** A product owner prioritizes customer value while operations prioritizes stability. The team needs an explicit decision framework and product or governance authority.

**Resource disagreement:** Two project leaders need the same specialist. The decision may require portfolio prioritization rather than interpersonal compromise.

**Safety concern:** A construction lead believes a proposed sequence creates a hazard. The project manager must pause affected work if needed, confirm the safety constraint, and involve the authorized safety role.

**Ethical concern:** A sponsor asks the project manager to conceal a material forecast variance. The project manager must not negotiate away honesty or reporting obligations.

3. Fairness.
4. Honesty.

### Narrator

“PMI identifies responsibility, respect, fairness, and honesty as values that drive ethical conduct. Conflict management is where those values become visible.”

### Ethical behaviors

1. Represent facts accurately.
2. Disclose relevant interests and constraints.
3. Listen without humiliation or retaliation.
4. Avoid discrimination, favoritism, and personal attacks.
5. Accept responsibility for decisions and consequences.
6. Preserve confidentiality where required.
7. Escalate misconduct through appropriate channels.
8. Do not manufacture consensus by suppressing dissent.

### Original scenario

A senior sponsor repeatedly interrupts a junior analyst who is presenting evidence that the launch forecast is unrealistic. Other participants stop contributing.

### Learner question

“What should the project manager do?”

### Strong response

Reinforce the meeting ground rules, ensure the evidence is heard, protect respectful participation, and move the discussion toward facts and decision authority. If the behavior persists or involves retaliation or misconduct, use the appropriate escalation path.

### Narrator

“Respectful conflict is not passive. The project manager protects the conditions required for honest evidence and professional disagreement.”

# Sequence 6: Resolution Strategies as Contextual Choices

## Timecode 16:10 to 20:30

### Picture

Five resolution pathways appear around the conflict diagnostic engine. The lesson describes them in original language without reproducing a copyrighted table.

### Pathway 1: Collaborate and solve jointly

Use when parties can work through interests, evidence, constraints, and options to create a durable solution.

Strong conditions:

1. Important issue.
2. Adequate time.
3. Relevant expertise is distributed.
4. Relationship and shared ownership matter.
5. A better integrated option may exist.

Risk:

Collaboration can become endless discussion when authority, urgency, or criteria are unclear.

### Pathway 2: Negotiate a tradeoff

Use when interests differ and each side can make authorized concessions.

Strong conditions:

1. A balanced agreement is acceptable.
2. Parties understand what may and may not be traded.
3. Time is limited but not an emergency.

Risk:

A compromise may create a weak solution if nonnegotiable safety, ethics, compliance, or quality requirements are treated as bargaining positions.

### Pathway 3: Decide or direct within authority

Use when the accountable role has authority, the evidence is sufficient, and delay creates harm or paralysis.

Strong conditions:

1. Decision rights are explicit.
2. The decision does not bypass required review.
3. The rationale and consequences are documented.

Risk:

Direction used too early can suppress expertise and ownership.

### Pathway 4: Defer or pause intentionally

Use when more evidence, an authorized participant, or a safer environment is required.

Strong conditions:

1. The pause is time bounded.
2. Evidence needs and owner are defined.
3. Interim risk controls are established.

Risk:

Unstructured delay becomes avoidance.

### Pathway 5: Escalate

Use when impact, authority, policy, unresolved conflict, misconduct, or material risk exceeds the team's decision rights.

Strong conditions:

1. The escalation includes facts, options, recommendation, urgency, and consequence.
2. The appropriate authority receives it.
3. The team does not use escalation to avoid ordinary collaboration.

### Narrator

“The exam usually rewards the lowest appropriate level of resolution, but not at the expense of safety, ethics, compliance, or required authority.”

7. Response time.
8. Documentation.
9. Confidentiality.
10. Inclusion and accessibility.
11. Conflict of interest.
12. Retaliation prohibition.

### Narrator

“Ground rules establish how the team will disagree before the disagreement becomes personal or disruptive. They should be created with the team, visible, enforceable, and updated when the operating environment changes.”

### Original example

A hybrid team adopts the following decision rule.

# Sequence 8: Predictive, Adaptive, and Hybrid Conflict Patterns

## Timecode 23:50 to 27:00

### Picture

The command table divides into three delivery environments.

### Predictive conflict pattern

A design change affects the approved baseline, contract, schedule, and compliance plan. The project manager facilitates analysis, but the change follows the authorized control process.

### Adaptive conflict pattern

The product owner and team disagree about backlog priority. The project manager or agile leader helps surface value, evidence, constraints, and product authority. The answer is not automatically to create a formal change request for every backlog decision.

### Hybrid conflict pattern

An adaptive team can replan iteration work, but a change affects an external regulatory milestone shared with a predictive workstream. The conflict crosses a governance boundary and requires integrated analysis and the authorized change path.

### Narrator

“The delivery approach changes the decision mechanism, but professional conduct, evidence, and accountable authority remain necessary.”

### Exam cues

1. Identify the delivery approach before selecting the process.
2. Do not force predictive control onto delegated adaptive decisions.
3. Do not use agility as a reason to bypass compliance, contract, security, or shared integration commitments.
4. Resolve interpersonal disagreement directly where appropriate.
5. Use governance when the decision exceeds delegated authority.

# Sequence 9: Examination Reasoning and Distractor Analysis

## Timecode 27:00 to 31:20

### Picture

The release decision from the cold open returns. The four positions are now organized into facts, interests, authority, options, and constraints.

### Original practice question 1

During an iteration, a product owner and security architect disagree about releasing a feature with an unresolved control weakness. The team has stopped discussing the issue and expects the project manager to decide. What should the project manager do first?

1. Choose the security architect's position because security always overrides product decisions.
2. Facilitate analysis of the conflict, evidence, decision rights, and applicable release constraints.
3. Escalate directly to the chief executive officer.
4. Release the feature and record the weakness as technical debt.

### Correct reasoning

Option 2 is strongest. The project manager first clarifies the facts, risk, applicable constraints, and decision authority. The final decision may require an authorized security, risk, compliance, product, or governance role, but automatic override and premature escalation bypass analysis.

### Distractor wall

**Option 1:** Assumes authority without confirming the governance model.  
**Option 3:** Escalates beyond the lowest appropriate level before diagnosis.  
**Option 4:** Accepts risk and changes release posture without authorization or verification.

### Original practice question 2

Two construction leads disagree about sequencing. Their argument is delaying work, and one proposed sequence may create a safety exposure. What should the project manager do first?

1. Ask the leads to compromise and continue work.
2. Pause affected work as necessary, confirm the safety constraint, and facilitate resolution with the authorized safety role.
3. Allow the more experienced lead to decide.
4. Wait until the next status meeting.

### Correct reasoning

Option 2 is strongest. Potential harm changes urgency and authority. The project manager protects people, verifies the constraint, and uses the appropriate role and process.

### Original multiple response practice

Which two actions strengthen conflict management before a dispute occurs?

1. Establish shared ground rules and decision paths.
2. Require the project manager to decide every disagreement.
3. Define evidence, escalation, and documentation expectations.
4. Prevent team members from challenging senior stakeholders.
5. Avoid documenting conflict because it may harm morale.

Correct responses: 1 and 3.

### Exam cue

When a question asks what to do first, look for diagnosis, direct engagement, evidence, and the appropriate authority before choosing avoidance, unilateral action, or escalation. When safety, ethics, or compliance may be affected, protect against harm and involve the authorized role.
