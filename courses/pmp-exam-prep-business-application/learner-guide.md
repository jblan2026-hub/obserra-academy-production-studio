# 3. The Obserra exam reasoning method

Use **DOMAIN–TASK–CONTEXT–ACTION**.

## Domain

Which broad area is involved?

- People
- Process
- Business Environment

## Task

What responsibility is being tested? Examples include leading a team, engaging stakeholders, planning delivery, managing schedule, establishing governance, controlling change, or managing risk.

## Context

Extract only the facts:

- Delivery approach
- Project phase
- Authority
- Stakeholders
- Evidence
- Urgency
- Risk
- Compliance
- Value
- Constraints

Do not add facts that are not in the scenario.

## Action

Choose the action that:

- addresses the actual problem;
- respects authority and ethics;
- engages the right people;
- uses evidence;
- follows the appropriate process;
- protects stakeholder value;
- avoids unnecessary escalation or premature execution.

# 4. Common weak-answer patterns

Be cautious when an option:

- replaces or disciplines a team member before understanding the cause;
- escalates immediately when the project manager has not analyzed or engaged stakeholders;
- changes a baseline without authorization;
- implements a sponsor request without impact analysis;
- hides or delays bad news;
- bypasses a process solely to meet a date;
- assumes agile means no plan or documentation;
- assumes predictive means no feedback or adaptation;
- treats a risk as though it has already occurred;
- treats an issue as though it is still uncertain;
- promises compliance or certification without evidence;
- treats a project output as proof of business value;
- chooses a tool before defining the problem.

Exceptions exist. Immediate action may be required for life safety, ethics, security containment, legal obligations, or authority-specific emergencies. Read the context.

# 6. Facts and public cases

The course uses documented cases to make concepts concrete.

## NASA major projects portfolio

GAO reported that NASA planned approximately 74 billion dollars in major-project investment for fiscal year 2025. Four of 18 assessed major projects had cost overruns and three had schedule delays during the assessed year. GAO also reported that three of 53 historical projects accounted for almost half of total overruns in its analysis.

**Lesson:** portfolio exposure can be concentrated. Leaders should identify which projects drive the largest value, risk, cost, and schedule consequences.

**Limitation:** NASA’s major-project portfolio is not representative of every industry or organization.

## Propulsion module planning case

GAO reported that inadequate planning and design contributed to a project’s failure to meet performance, cost, and schedule goals. The later assessment defined success criteria, identified assumptions, and compared alternatives.

**Lesson:** disciplined success criteria, assumptions, and alternatives analysis improve decision quality.

**Limitation:** this is a historical government acquisition case and should not be treated as a universal causal model.

## GAO schedule guidance

GAO identifies ten best practices for a reliable, high-quality schedule. An integrated schedule helps leaders determine whether milestones are realistic and understand how changes affect the overall program.

**Lesson:** a schedule is a model of work and dependencies, not a list of preferred dates.

## GAO cost guidance

GAO provides a structured methodology for reliable cost estimates and applies the guidance across software, infrastructure, spacecraft, networks, construction, rail, census, and other programs.

**Lesson:** cost estimates require defined scope, data, assumptions, methods, risk analysis, documentation, and updates.
