## Scene 1. Cold open: the report was accurate and still failed, 00:09 to 02:35

**VISUAL**

A project status report contains 42 pages of accurate data.

The executive committee receives it twelve minutes before the meeting.

The first page shows a green overall status.

Deep inside the report:

1. A supplier milestone is four weeks late.
2. The critical path now has three days of float.
3. A risk has exceeded the escalation threshold.
4. Customer adoption is below target.
5. The cost forecast assumes approval that has not occurred.
6. The decision needed from executives is not stated.

The committee reviews the green status and moves to the next topic.

**NARRATOR**

The data was accurate. The communication did not support the decision.

Effective project communication delivers the right evidence, to the right stakeholder, through the right channel, at the right time, with the right level of context, protection, and required action.

## Scene 3. Communication begins with a decision need, 03:20 to 06:35

**VISUAL**

A communication design model appears.

1. Purpose.
2. Audience.
3. Decision or action needed.
4. Evidence and context.
5. Channel and format.
6. Timing and frequency.
7. Owner and sender.
8. Security and classification.
9. Feedback and confirmation.
10. Record and retention.

**NARRATOR**

The project manager should begin with the question:

> What must this stakeholder understand, decide, do, or verify?

A report designed for audit evidence is not the same as an executive decision briefing. A technical defect record is not the same as a customer communication. A daily team synchronization is not the same as a regulatory notice.

**ON SCREEN PRINCIPLE**

> Communication quality is measured by decision and action effectiveness, not by volume.

## Scene 4. Data, information, evidence, and intelligence, 06:35 to 09:45

**VISUAL**

Four layers form.

### Data

Raw observations, values, events, and records.

### Information

Data organized to provide meaning and context.

### Evidence

Information with source, integrity, relevance, timing, confidence, and limitations sufficient to support a conclusion or decision.

### Decision intelligence

Evidence synthesized with options, impact, authority, trade offs, recommendations, and required action.

**NARRATOR**

A dashboard can contain data without evidence. A report can contain evidence without identifying the decision. A recommendation can appear confident while relying on stale or incomplete sources.

A strong report states:

1. What is known.
2. What is assumed.
3. What has changed.
4. Why it matters.
5. Which options exist.
6. Which authority is needed.
7. What is recommended and why.
8. What remains uncertain.
9. How the outcome will be verified.

## Scene 5. Tailoring by audience, 09:45 to 12:50

**VISUAL**

One project issue is communicated to five audiences.

### Delivery team

Detailed actions, dependencies, owners, and immediate coordination.

### Sponsor

Outcome, exposure, options, recommendation, authority, and escalation need.

### Customer

Impact, current service, expected resolution, commitments, and support channel.

### Regulator or auditor

Required facts, evidence, controls, timing, and records under the applicable obligation.

### Board or executive committee

Enterprise impact, material uncertainty, strategic options, risk appetite, financial context, and decision.

**NARRATOR**

Tailoring does not mean changing the facts. It means selecting relevant detail and format without concealing material information.

**ETHICS CONNECTION**

Honesty prohibits misleading omission. Responsibility requires timely escalation. Respect requires accessible communication. Fairness requires that affected stakeholders receive information appropriate to their role and impact.

## Scene 6. Decision ready status reporting, 12:50 to 16:25

**VISUAL**

The 42 page report from the cold open becomes a two level reporting system.

### Executive first page

1. Outcome and current forecast.
2. Status by evidence, not color alone.
3. Material changes since last report.
4. Top risks and issues with owners.
5. Decisions and approvals required.
6. Financial and schedule impact.
7. Confidence and data limitations.
8. Next verification point.

### Evidence annex

1. Integrated schedule.
2. Cost and forecast data.
3. Risk and issue records.
4. Quality evidence.
5. Stakeholder and adoption measures.
6. Change history.
7. Source and calculation details.

**NARRATOR**

Executives need enough context to make a responsible decision and a path to inspect the evidence.

**ON SCREEN STATUS RULES**

1. Define status thresholds.
2. Show observation date and source.
3. Explain trend and forecast.
4. Identify missing data.
5. Avoid averaging away concentrated risk.
6. State the required action.
7. Preserve access to supporting evidence.

## Scene 7. Feedback and closed loop communication, 16:25 to 19:10

**VISUAL**

A message moves through a closed loop.

Send.  
Receive.  
Interpret.  
Confirm.  
Act.  
Verify.  
Adjust.

**NARRATOR**

Communication is not complete when a message is sent.

Closed loop communication confirms that the intended recipient received the message, understood the meaning, accepted or challenged the action, and completed or escalated the response.

Examples include:

1. Read back of a high consequence instruction.
2. Confirmation of a change decision and effective date.
3. Stakeholder feedback with a documented response.
4. Training followed by observed performance.
5. An incident action followed by independent verification.
6. A report decision followed by assigned actions and completion evidence.

**PSYCHOLOGICAL SAFETY CONNECTION**

Feedback loops require people to report misunderstanding, error, or uncertainty. Leaders should respond productively so the project receives accurate information rather than silent compliance.

## Scene 8. Documented public case: reports without all user input, 19:10 to 23:20

**VISUAL**

A source labeled government modernization case appears.

**SOURCE CARD**

U.S. Government Accountability Office  
Electronic Health Records: VA Needs to Address Data Management Challenges for New System  
GAO 22 103718  
Published 1 February 2022  
Source ID: GAO VA EHR DATA USERS 2022

**NARRATOR**

GAO reported data-quality challenges in the Department of Veterans Affairs' new electronic health record system. GAO also reported that VA began making system reports available without consulting all users and therefore could not be sure that the reports met all user needs.

This case demonstrates that a report can be technically produced and still fail as a stakeholder product.

A project should ask:

1. Who uses the report?
2. Which decisions must it support?
3. Which definitions and data sources are required?
4. What timing and format fit the work?
5. Which users were not consulted?
6. How will accuracy, usability, and completeness be validated?

**SOURCE CARD TWO**

U.S. Government Accountability Office  
Electronic Health Records: Challenges with VA's New System Call for Management Improvements  
GAO 23 106785  
Published 9 May 2023  
Source ID: GAO VA EHR CHANGE MANAGEMENT 2023

**NARRATOR**

GAO also reported that many users said they were not adequately trained to use the new system and that VA had not established goals to assess user satisfaction.

Knowledge transfer requires more than delivering training. It requires defined capability, practice, support, performance evidence, and feedback.

**LIMITATIONS CARD**

1. Federal healthcare modernization context.
2. The reports describe a complex program with many contributing factors.
3. User consultation and training are not the only determinants of outcome.
4. The findings should not be generalized without context.
5. Learners should consult the official reports before professional use.

## Scene 10. Communication and knowledge risk, 26:25 to 28:25

**VISUAL**

A knowledge concentration map reveals several single points of failure.

1. One architect knows the integration rationale.
2. One analyst maintains the cost model.
3. One vendor technician knows the recovery sequence.
4. One executive assistant knows the stakeholder history.
5. One engineer knows the temporary workaround.

**NARRATOR**

Knowledge concentration is a project and operational risk.

The project manager should identify critical knowledge holders, transfer timing, successor roles, access controls, documentation, practice, and verification before transition or departure.

**PRIVACY AND SECURITY WARNING**

Knowledge transfer must follow least privilege, confidentiality, intellectual property, export, privacy, and records requirements. Transfer the knowledge required for authorized work, not unrestricted access.

## Scene 11. Original enterprise scenario: Aegis Control Center, 28:25 to 31:15

**LABEL**

Obserra original synthetic scenario. Not a documented public case.

**SCENARIO**

Aegis is deploying a security operations platform.

1. The executive dashboard reports 92 percent readiness.
2. The incident team cannot explain how the score is calculated.
3. One vendor engineer knows the emergency recovery process.
4. The operations team completed online training but has not practiced failover.
5. The sponsor believes the platform is ready for production.
6. The project manager's report does not identify a decision or limitation.

**QUESTION**

What should the project manager do next?

**OPTIONS**

A. Approve launch because readiness exceeds 90 percent.  
B. Add more detail to the dashboard without changing the decision process.  
C. Validate the readiness measure and source, disclose limitations, perform knowledge and failover verification, address concentration risk, and present the accountable launch decision with evidence.  
D. Ask the vendor to own all operational knowledge.

**NARRATOR**

Option C is strongest. The project needs decision quality and verified capability, not a persuasive number.

A accepts an unexplained metric. B increases volume without solving evidence or capability gaps. D creates vendor dependence and weakens organizational ownership.

**FOLLOW UP**

The team conducts a failover simulation, documents the recovery sequence, trains two internal roles, corrects the readiness model, and changes the executive report to show confidence, limitations, and the launch decision required.

## Scene 12. Common PMP communication traps, 31:15 to 33:00

### Trap 1. Send more communication

First identify the audience, decision, evidence, timing, and feedback need.

### Trap 2. Use the same report for everyone

Tailor format and detail while preserving material facts.

### Trap 3. Hide uncertainty to appear confident

State assumptions, confidence, and limitations honestly.

### Trap 4. Treat training completion as competence

Verify that people can perform the required work.

### Trap 5. Capture lessons only at closure

Capture and apply lessons throughout the project.

### Trap 6. Store documents and call it knowledge transfer

Validate understanding, practice, and independent performance.

### Trap 7. Make a dashboard color the decision

Define source, threshold, owner, confidence, consequence, and required action.

## Knowledge check package

### Question 1

An executive report is accurate but does not state the decision required. What should the project manager do?

A. Add more pages.  
B. Reframe the report around outcome, change, evidence, options, authority, recommendation, uncertainty, and the decision needed.  
C. Remove the risk data.  
D. Send the same report more frequently.

**Correct response:** B.

### Question 2

Users complete training but cannot perform a critical recovery procedure. What should the project manager do?

A. Mark knowledge transfer complete because attendance is recorded.  
B. Provide practice, verify performance, address gaps, and retain evidence before transition.  
C. Ask users to sign a waiver.  
D. Transfer the responsibility to the vendor.

**Correct response:** B.

### Question 3

Which action best closes a communication loop?

A. Sending an email.  
B. Confirming receipt, interpretation, action ownership, and completed response.  
C. Adding recipients.  
D. Saving the message.

**Correct response:** B.

### Question 4

A dashboard reports 92 percent readiness but the calculation is not understood. What should the project manager do?

A. Accept the score.  
B. Validate the metric definition, source, threshold, confidence, limitations, and decision use.  
C. Change the color to yellow.  
D. Remove the metric.

**Correct response:** B.

## Closing summary, 33:00 to 34:10

**NARRATOR**

Project communication should move evidence into responsible decisions and actions. Tailor the message, not the truth. State what is known, assumed, changed, required, and uncertain. Confirm understanding and close the action loop.

Knowledge transfer should create verified capability, not merely documents or attendance. Identify critical knowledge, transfer it through practice, preserve it securely, and test whether the receiving team can perform.

On the PMP exam, when communication fails, do not simply communicate more. Diagnose the audience, purpose, decision, evidence, channel, timing, feedback, and authority.

**ON SCREEN**

> Communicate for decisions. Verify understanding. Transfer capability. Preserve learning.
