# Obserra Academy AI Native Evidence Grounded Course Standard

## Purpose

This standard governs every new or materially revised Obserra Academy course. It replaces uniform, generic curriculum generation with a course specific production model. Course duration, lesson count, prerequisites, outcomes, assessment requirements, and completion conditions must match the approved course specification. A course must never be forced into a standard number of modules or lessons merely for production convenience.

The standard applies to course manifests, instructor manuscripts, learner guides, workbooks, assessments, AI tutor profiles, video scripts, visual production plans, certificates, catalog records, and learner access experiences.

## Source of truth and traceability

Every course must identify an approved source of truth. For an existing public course, the approved Obserra Academy catalog record and course page control the title, duration, lesson count, audience, description, stated requirements, outcomes, and commercial representation. For a new course, the Academy Production Studio manifest becomes the controlled source and the public site may publish only after owner approval and release evidence.

Each lesson must include a traceability record connecting:

1. Course outcome.
2. Lesson objective.
3. Authoritative source or approved original doctrine.
4. Facts and data used in instruction.
5. Practical example or documented public case.
6. Business application exercise.
7. Assessment objective.
8. AI tutor knowledge boundary.
9. Video script and visual evidence plan.
10. Review and release evidence.

Unsupported facts, invented regulatory requirements, fabricated statistics, invented case studies presented as real, and unverified certification claims are prohibited.

## Instructional depth

Every substantive lesson must answer six questions.

1. What is the concept?
2. Why does it matter?
3. What authoritative requirement, standard, guidance, research, or doctrine supports it?
4. What facts, data, assumptions, and limitations should the learner understand?
5. How is the concept applied in a real organization?
6. How should the learner recognize and answer a related assessment or scenario question?

Lessons must distinguish law, regulation, contractual obligation, consensus standard, framework, professional guidance, internal policy, recommended practice, and instructor interpretation. These categories are not interchangeable.

## Authoritative source hierarchy

Sources should be selected in this order when available:

1. Statutes, regulations, official government publications, court opinions, and regulator guidance.
2. Official standards bodies and certification owner publications.
3. Official professional association standards, codes, exam outlines, and practice guidance.
4. Government audit and oversight reports.
5. Peer reviewed research and authoritative academic publications.
6. Official corporate disclosures, public post incident reports, and verified case studies.
7. Reputable secondary analysis used only when a primary source is unavailable.

Each course must maintain an authoritative source register with source owner, title, publication date, retrieval date, jurisdiction, source type, URL, lesson mappings, and usage limitations. Copyrighted standards may be summarized and cited but must not be reproduced beyond permitted quotation limits or license terms.

## Facts and data

Every lesson must provide meaningful factual density. At minimum, a lesson must contain:

1. Three verified facts or quantitative data points where the subject supports quantitative treatment.
2. Two authoritative source mappings.
3. One practical business artifact, calculation, decision model, or implementation procedure.
4. One documented public case or clearly labeled synthetic scenario.
5. One limitations statement explaining what the evidence does not prove.

Facts must be current as of the source verification date. Time sensitive facts must display the observation date. Course materials must not imply that historical statistics are current.

## Quotations and legal material

Direct quotations must be short, necessary, accurately reproduced, and attributed. U.S. government text may be quoted where appropriate, but learners must still be directed to the controlling official source. Copyrighted standards, certification materials, books, and examination content must be paraphrased unless Obserra has a license permitting broader use.

A lesson must never reproduce confidential examination questions, exam dumps, copyrighted test banks, or proprietary courseware. Independent exam preparation must teach the published examination objectives, professional knowledge, reasoning patterns, and original practice questions.

## Practical and documented examples

Each lesson must include both forms of application:

1. A documented public example, where a reliable source exists, explaining what happened, what the evidence establishes, what decisions were made, and what lessons may reasonably be drawn.
2. An Obserra original scenario that allows the learner to practice the concept in a realistic business environment.

Examples must not oversimplify causation. A project outcome usually has multiple technical, organizational, financial, governance, and environmental contributors. The course should identify uncertainty and avoid claiming that a single method would have guaranteed a different result.

## AI native learner support

Every paid course must include a course specific AI tutor that becomes available only after the learner has a valid entitlement for that course. The tutor must receive the learner's authorized course context, current lesson, progress, completed assessments, missed concepts, source register, and approved instructional content.

The tutor may:

1. Explain a concept at different levels of depth.
2. Provide additional original examples.
3. Compare methods and decision options.
4. Generate a personalized study plan.
5. Conduct Socratic questioning and oral review.
6. Generate original ungraded practice questions.
7. Explain why an answer is stronger or weaker.
8. Coach business application using learner supplied nonrestricted context.
9. Recommend review based on observed learning gaps.
10. Cite the approved course sources supporting its answer.

The tutor must not:

1. Reveal answers during a graded assessment.
2. claim legal, regulatory, certification, medical, financial, engineering, or compliance authority.
3. invent source support.
4. expose content from courses the learner has not purchased.
5. train on restricted learner or organizational data without explicit approved governance.
6. modify its own governing policy or course source set.

Continuous adaptation means governed personalization based on progress, practice performance, feedback, and recurring learning gaps. It does not mean uncontrolled self modification. Recommendations must remain explainable, attributable, reversible, and reviewable.

## Cinematic video production standard

Every lesson must have a video production package before release. A package contains:

1. Learning objective and source map.
2. Final narration script.
3. Scene by scene storyboard.
4. Shot list and visual asset list.
5. On screen facts and data graphics.
6. Source cards for every factual claim.
7. Case study visualization.
8. Original business scenario.
9. Knowledge check transition.
10. Captions, transcript, and audio description plan.
11. Brand, ownership, disclaimer, and classification overlays.
12. Rights and licensing record for every asset.

The target visual quality is premium documentary and cinematic corporate training. The visual language uses black, dark navy, gold, restrained holographic blue, high contrast typography, realistic enterprise environments, data driven motion graphics, and the owner approved Obserra mark. Cinematic quality must support comprehension rather than obscure it.

Minimum production target:

1. 3840 by 2160 master or an approved equivalent production master.
2. 23.976 or 24 frames per second for narrative scenes, with approved higher frame rate capture when needed.
3. 48 kHz professional audio.
4. Loudness normalized for online training delivery.
5. Accurate captions and downloadable transcript.
6. Accessible color contrast and reduced motion alternative.
7. No unlicensed music, footage, photographs, logos, or likenesses.
8. No unsupported AI generated depiction presented as documentary evidence.

AI generated visuals may be used as illustrative reenactments only when clearly labeled. Real people, organizations, incidents, and statistics must be represented accurately and respectfully.

## Video narrative structure

A normal lesson video should use this sequence:

1. Cinematic cold open presenting a consequential decision.
2. Lesson objective and why the topic matters.
3. Authoritative standard, regulation, guidance, or doctrine.
4. Fact and data sequence with source attribution.
5. Documented public case.
6. Original business application scenario.
7. Decision analysis showing strong and weak choices.
8. Exam or assessment reasoning pattern where applicable.
9. Summary, limitations, and next action.
10. Transition to the AI tutor, workbook, and knowledge check.

## Assessment standard

Assessment questions must measure application, judgment, evidence interpretation, and decision quality rather than superficial recall. Each question record must include:

1. Objective and lesson mapping.
2. Correct answer.
3. Rationale for the correct answer.
4. Explanation for each distractor.
5. Source references.
6. Difficulty.
7. Cognitive level.
8. Review status.
9. Originality attestation.

Exam preparation courses must use original questions aligned to the public examination outline. They must not claim that an original practice question appeared or will appear on a certification examination.

## Branding, ownership, and disclaimers

Every learner surface must use the official Obserra visual system and owner approved logo. Required labels include, as applicable:

1. Property of Obserra.
2. Obserra proprietary information. Not for distribution.
3. Paid access content.
4. AI native course.
5. Certificate eligible.
6. Internal review or public release approved.
7. Final assessment protected.

The approved informational use disclaimer, assumption of risk, release, warranty disclaimer, and limitation of liability must appear in every placement required by the official brand policy.

## Course review gates

A course cannot be represented as complete until the following gates pass:

1. Source of truth reconciliation.
2. Subject matter review.
3. Technical and factual verification.
4. Legal and regulatory review when applicable.
5. Copyright and trademark review.
6. Brand review.
7. Accessibility review.
8. Assessment quality review.
9. AI tutor safety and grounding review.
10. Video rights, caption, transcript, and production quality review.
11. Commerce and entitlement review.
12. Release packaging and rollback review.

## Migration of existing courses

Existing courses must be migrated course by course. The migration process must not retain a generic lesson count or generic module template when it conflicts with the approved catalog. Each migrated course receives an exact specification, source register, lesson architecture, AI tutor profile, video production plan, assessment map, and release evidence.

PMP Exam Preparation and Business Application is the first course implemented under this standard.
