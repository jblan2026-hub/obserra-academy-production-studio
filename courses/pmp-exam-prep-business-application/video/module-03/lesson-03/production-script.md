## Scene 3. Conflict is information, 03:15 to 06:25

**VISUAL**

A conflict signal opens into six possible sources.

### Task conflict

Disagreement about the work, evidence, requirements, solution, quality, or technical approach.

### Process conflict

Disagreement about how decisions are made, how work is organized, or how resources are allocated.

### Relationship conflict

Interpersonal tension, disrespect, mistrust, identity threat, or accumulated history.

### Resource conflict

Competition for people, money, time, equipment, data, access, or executive attention.

### Value and priority conflict

Different definitions of success, risk tolerance, ethics, stakeholder value, or strategic importance.

### Authority conflict

Unclear or contested decision rights, ownership, approval, or escalation.

**NARRATOR**

The visible argument may not be the actual conflict.

A schedule dispute may be a resource conflict. A technical dispute may be a risk appetite conflict. A personality conflict may be the result of repeated process unfairness. A stakeholder objection may reveal an unaddressed impact.

**ON SCREEN PRINCIPLE**

> Treat conflict as evidence about the project system before treating it as a defect in the people.

## Scene 4. Conflict stage and consequence, 06:25 to 09:25

**VISUAL**

A conflict develops through five stages.

1. Latent conditions.
2. Perceived difference.
3. Felt tension.
4. Visible behavior.
5. Outcome and memory.

**NARRATOR**

Early conflict may be resolved through clarification. Later conflict may require formal facilitation, governance, or organizational intervention.

The project manager should assess:

1. Urgency.
2. Material impact.
3. Safety and legal implications.
4. Power imbalance.
5. Emotional intensity.
6. Evidence quality.
7. Relationship history.
8. Decision authority.
9. Whether the parties can participate safely and fairly.
10. Whether the conflict has affected delivery, trust, or stakeholder harm.

**ESCALATION WARNING**

Conflict involving threats, harassment, discrimination, retaliation, violence, fraud, safety, or legal concerns should be routed through approved organizational processes. Do not attempt to privately mediate matters beyond project authority or competence.

## Scene 5. Positions, interests, evidence, and authority, 09:25 to 12:45

**VISUAL**

A conflict statement is decomposed.

### Position

“We must centralize the platform.”

### Interest

Reduce cost, simplify operations, and create one release path.

### Evidence

Current cost, operating performance, technical dependencies, and support data.

### Assumptions

Regional requirements can be met within one architecture.

### Constraints

Regulation, resilience, contract, security, time, and budget.

### Authority

The architecture board recommends. The executive governance body approves material operating-model risk.

**NARRATOR**

Parties may defend positions when their interests could be met through different options.

A project manager should surface:

1. What each party is trying to protect.
2. Which evidence supports the concern.
3. Which assumptions differ.
4. Which criteria should govern the decision.
5. Who has authority to decide.
6. What happens if no agreement is reached.

**ORIGINAL FACILITATOR QUESTION**

“If we did not begin with either proposed design, which outcomes, risks, and constraints would the final option need to satisfy?”

## Scene 6. Resolution strategies, 12:45 to 17:05

**VISUAL**

Six strategies appear on a context map.

### Collaborate or problem solve

Use when the issue is important, time permits meaningful analysis, relationships matter, and integrated expertise can create a better solution.

### Compromise or reconcile

Use when parties need a workable agreement, time or resources are limited, and each can accept part of the outcome.

### Accommodate or smooth

Use when the issue matters more to the other party, preserving the relationship is important, or the project manager recognizes that another position is stronger.

### Avoid or withdraw

Use temporarily when the issue is low value, information is missing, emotions need time to stabilize, or another forum is more appropriate. Avoidance is not resolution when the issue remains material.

### Direct or force

Use when immediate action is required, authority is clear, and safety, compliance, incident response, or time critical consequence demands a decision. Direction should still be documented and reviewed.

### Escalate

Use when the decision exceeds authority, parties cannot resolve a material issue, risk exceeds tolerance, or ethical, legal, safety, or governance concerns require another owner.

**NARRATOR**

No strategy is universally best. Collaboration is often strong because it addresses causes and preserves commitment, but it can be inappropriate when immediate containment is required or when parties cannot safely participate.

**EXAM LENS**

1. How urgent is the issue?
2. What is at risk?
3. Can the team resolve it within authority?
4. Does the project manager need more evidence?
5. Which response addresses the cause and preserves accountability?

## Scene 7. Ground rules that operate under pressure, 17:05 to 19:45

**VISUAL**

A team working agreement moves from a static document into a live conflict discussion.

**STRONG GROUND RULES**

1. Challenge ideas and evidence, not identity or motive.
2. State facts, assumptions, uncertainty, and source.
3. Listen without interruption.
4. Identify decision criteria before arguing for an option.
5. Declare conflicts of interest.
6. Protect dissent and record unresolved concerns.
7. Respect time, confidentiality, and professional boundaries.
8. Follow the agreed decision and escalation process.
9. Confirm actions, owners, and deadlines.
10. Revisit the agreement after material conflict.

**NARRATOR**

Ground rules matter only when leaders model and enforce them consistently.

When a senior leader interrupts, dismisses, or attacks others without consequence, the written rules become symbolic.

**PSYCHOLOGICAL SAFETY CONNECTION**

Team learning requires people to ask questions, admit uncertainty, and challenge assumptions. Ground rules create structure for that interpersonal risk while preserving standards and respectful conduct.

## Scene 8. Documented public case: engineering data and management judgment, 19:45 to 24:20

**VISUAL**

A respectful, source labeled sequence based on the official Challenger investigation. No crew reenactment or sensational accident imagery.

**SOURCE CARD**

Report of the Presidential Commission on the Space Shuttle Challenger Accident, Volume 1  
Published 6 June 1986  
Source ID: NASA ROGERS COMMISSION 1986

**NARRATOR**

The Rogers Commission concluded that the decision process leading to the Challenger launch was flawed in several ways.

The report identified communication failures that resulted in a launch decision based on incomplete and misleading information. It identified conflict between engineering data and management judgments. It identified a management structure that permitted flight-safety problems to bypass key Shuttle managers.

The Commission reported that decision makers did not receive all material information, including the continuing opposition of engineers after a contractor management reversal.

**NARRATOR**

This was not an ordinary team disagreement. It involved high consequence technical evidence, management pressure, authority, communication, and safety governance.

The project leadership lesson is not that every technical concern automatically controls the decision. The lesson is that material dissent must reach authorized decision makers with its evidence and uncertainty intact.

**LIMITATIONS AND RESPECT CARD**

1. The accident resulted in loss of life and must be treated respectfully.
2. The Commission identified multiple technical and organizational contributors.
3. Conflict-management terminology does not replace the Commission's formal findings.
4. The case must not be reduced to one conversation or personality.
5. Learners should consult the official report before professional use.

## Scene 9. Original enterprise scenario: Nova Architecture Decision, 24:20 to 28:35

**LABEL**

Obserra original synthetic scenario. Not a documented public case.

**SCENARIO**

Nova is selecting an architecture for a global payments platform.

1. The platform lead favors centralized processing for cost and speed.
2. The regional risk lead favors isolation for resilience and local obligations.
3. Both leaders have valid evidence.
4. Their discussion has become personal.
5. The architecture board is authorized to recommend but not accept material regulatory risk.
6. The executive governance body owns the final risk decision.
7. The sponsor tells the project manager to “make them compromise today.”

**QUESTION**

What should the project manager do next?

**OPTIONS**

A. Select the lowest cost option.  
B. Ask each leader to give up half of the design.  
C. Restore ground rules, separate positions from interests and evidence, establish decision criteria, facilitate option analysis, and escalate unresolved material risk to the authorized governance body.  
D. Remove both leaders from the project.

**NARRATOR**

Option C is strongest. The conflict contains valuable evidence and a decision-rights boundary.

A bypasses risk and authority. B assumes that a midpoint is technically or legally valid. D uses personnel action before cause analysis and organizational process.

**FOLLOW UP**

The team creates a hybrid option with centralized services and regional controls. Some risk remains. The architecture board recommends the option, the risk owner provides analysis, and executive governance makes the authorized decision.

The project manager records the decision, dissent, conditions, actions, and verification plan.

## Scene 10. Conflict closure and learning, 28:35 to 30:25

**VISUAL**

A conflict record closes through six steps.

1. Decision or agreement.
2. Rationale and evidence.
3. Unresolved concerns.
4. Actions and owners.
5. Relationship and ground-rule reset.
6. Verification and lessons.

**NARRATOR**

A decision does not automatically repair the working relationship.

The project manager should confirm:

1. What was decided.
2. How the parties will work together.
3. Whether any concern remains open.
4. What behavior must change.
5. How the decision will be tested.
6. When the team will review the outcome.

**ON SCREEN PRINCIPLE**

> Resolve the decision. Restore the working system. Learn from the conflict.

## Scene 11. Common PMP conflict traps, 30:25 to 32:20

### Trap 1. Escalate immediately

First understand and facilitate when the issue can be safely resolved within team authority.

### Trap 2. Avoid to preserve harmony

Avoidance can be temporary, but material conflict still needs resolution.

### Trap 3. Force a compromise

A midpoint may not satisfy technical, ethical, legal, or value requirements.

### Trap 4. Treat conflict as a personality defect

Analyze task, process, resource, value, and authority causes.

### Trap 5. Collaborate during an immediate safety event

When immediate authorized action is required, direct or contain first and review afterward.

### Trap 6. Hide dissent after a decision

Record material dissent and the evidence considered, especially for high consequence decisions.

## Knowledge check package

### Question 1

Two technical leaders disagree and both have valid evidence. The decision is not urgent. What should the project manager do first?

A. Escalate to the sponsor.  
B. Facilitate a fact based analysis of interests, assumptions, criteria, and authority.  
C. Select the senior leader's option.  
D. Avoid the issue.

**Correct response:** B.

### Question 2

When is a directing conflict strategy most appropriate?

A. When a low priority issue can wait.  
B. When immediate action is required, authority is clear, and safety, compliance, or time critical consequence demands a decision.  
C. Whenever the project manager disagrees.  
D. When all parties want more discussion.

**Correct response:** B.

### Question 3

A material technical concern exceeds the team's decision authority. What should the project manager do?

A. Suppress the concern to avoid delay.  
B. Preserve the evidence and dissent and escalate to the authorized decision owner.  
C. Ask the team to vote.  
D. Split the difference.

**Correct response:** B.

### Question 4

Which ground rule best supports constructive conflict?

A. Senior leaders speak first and last.  
B. Challenge ideas and evidence, state assumptions, and follow the agreed decision process.  
C. Avoid negative information.  
D. Every disagreement must be escalated.

**Correct response:** B.

## Closing summary, 32:20 to 33:30

**NARRATOR**

Conflict can reveal important differences in evidence, interests, values, resources, process, and authority. The project manager should diagnose before choosing a strategy.

Facilitate when parties can solve the issue. Compromise when a workable trade is appropriate. Accommodate deliberately. Avoid only when temporary withdrawal is justified. Direct when immediate authorized action is necessary. Escalate when authority, risk, ethics, safety, or unresolved barriers require it.

On the PMP exam, protect respectful dissent, use evidence, clarify authority, select the proportionate response, and close the learning loop.

**ON SCREEN**

> Diagnose the conflict. Protect the evidence. Govern the decision. Restore the team.
