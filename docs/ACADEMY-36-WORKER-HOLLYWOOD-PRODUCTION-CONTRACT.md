# Academy 36 Worker Hollywood Production Contract

**Document ID:** ACADEMY PROD CONTRACT 036 001  
**Status:** Owner approved temporary production surge contract  
**Applies to:** All owner review eligible Obserra Academy courses, protected learner packages, assessments, media production, accessibility assets, source records, certificates, compliance staging, and LCMS preparation  
**Publication authority:** Not granted by this contract

## 1. Purpose

This contract temporarily assigns all 36 logical workers in the governed Obserra production portfolio to Academy course production. Application development work is not authorized under this surge allocation. The purpose is to complete the learner facing substance of the Academy portfolio, including detailed instruction, learner materials, applied exercises, examinations, cinematic media packages, accessibility alternatives, reference traceability, applicability guidance, certificates of course completion, and compliance staging evidence.

The phrase Hollywood production quality means premium documentary and cinematic educational production planning and execution. It requires disciplined storytelling, scene level scripts, professional narration, intentional visual language, shot planning, source cards, sound direction, caption and transcript production, audio description, reduced motion alternatives, rights clearance, and technical quality control. It does not permit a storyboard, script, provider job, silent render, placeholder file, or unreviewed draft to be represented as a final mastered video.

## 2. Governed worker allocation

The authoritative temporary allocation is 36 Academy course workers, zero application workers, and 36 total portfolio workers. Every worker is interchangeable across the approved course production roles. A worker may move among instructional design, subject matter research, source verification, applicability mapping, assessment development, cinematic scriptwriting, storyboard and shot planning, narration, captions, transcripts, accessibility alternatives, rights and licensing, certificate design, quality assurance, compliance staging, and LCMS packaging according to the verified queue.

Interchangeability does not eliminate accountability. Every task must retain worker identity, assigned role, course identity, source hashes, checkpoint evidence, outcome, failure classification, and timestamp. Workers may not perform application development, publish a course, enable checkout, change pricing, bypass review, expand access, grant entitlements, or represent an incomplete artifact as final.

## 3. Course completion definition

A course is not complete merely because a manifest, outline, manuscript, assessment draft, video script, or generated package exists. A complete learner package requires original instructional content, substantive lesson narratives, learning objectives, executive and operational examples, realistic scenarios, applied exercises, knowledge checks, a governed final examination, learner workbook content, instructor guidance, source traceability, applicability guidance, media production assets, captions, transcripts, accessibility alternatives, rights evidence, certificate metadata, entitlement controls, progress and completion behavior, review evidence, rollback evidence, and owner acceptance.

Every course must remain marked as draft and must keep publication disabled until all final release gates pass. A successful authoring run proves protected package generation only. A successful compliance staging result proves structural conformance only. Neither result proves that the course is purchasable, published, accessible to learners, legally sufficient, independently verified, or operational in production.

## 4. Instructional depth standard

Each manifest module must be represented exactly once. Each module must contain a minimum 1,200 word substantive lesson narrative written for the defined audience and level. The narrative must use mature multi paragraph prose, explain the business and operational context, distinguish evidence from assumption, identify decision authority, describe escalation and documentation expectations, and show how the subject applies in practice.

Each module must include at least six learning objectives, six key concepts, an executive example, an operational example, a realistic scenario, an applied exercise that produces a reviewable learner artifact, four knowledge checks, ten slide narratives, and a complete scene level video script. Content must avoid repeated scenarios, repeated distractor patterns, unsupported statistics, fabricated events, invented quotations, invented legal requirements, and invented citations.

## 5. Reference and applicability contract

Every externally verifiable claim must map to a source record. Source records must identify the source title, issuing authority, source type, exact locator when supplied, publication or observation date when available, applicable jurisdictions, affected modules, claim topics, applicability, conditions under which the source applies, conditions under which it does not apply, limitations, verification status, verification instruction, and usage boundary.

Workers may use exact sources supplied in the governed course repository. When an exact source is not supplied, the worker must use the literal locator value `verification-required` and classify the record as requiring independent verification. Workers may never fabricate a URL, document number, standard clause, publication date, quotation, statistic, case fact, or source identifier.

Every course must include an applicability matrix that explains where each cited authority or practice applies. The matrix must identify industries, roles, jurisdictions, organization conditions, decision owners, implementation dependencies, limitations, applies when conditions, and does not apply when conditions. Framework mappings are informational only and may not be described as certification, legal sufficiency, attestation, audit evidence, authorization to operate, or proof of compliance.

## 6. Cinematic media contract

Every instructional module must include a premium documentary and cinematic treatment. The treatment must include a creative intent, cinematic cold open, coherent story arc, at least eight scenes, at least eight planned shots, source cards, narration intent, on screen text, visual direction, sound design, music boundaries, transitions, continuity notes, rights requirements, and accessibility alternatives.

Every video script must include scene level professional narration, visuals, on screen text, source identifiers, audio cues, caption text, alternate descriptions, an opening, a closing, a caption plan, a transcript plan, an audio description plan, and a reduced motion alternative. Final release requires mastered video, verified audible narration, 48 kHz or owner approved equivalent audio, loudness verification, caption files, final transcripts, audio description or an approved equivalent, source cards, rights records, and technical quality control.

Music, imagery, footage, logos, photographs, diagrams, sound effects, voice assets, avatars, and third party materials may not enter final release without documented rights or an approved original asset record. Synthetic media and reenactments must be labeled when the distinction matters.

## 7. Assessment contract

Every course must include an assessment blueprint with module coverage, cognitive level targets, question type targets, integrity requirements, and psychometric review requirements. The final examination must contain at least 30 original questions unless a course specific contract requires more. Questions must emphasize application, analysis, prioritization, evidence evaluation, escalation, and defensible judgment.

Every question must include a correct answer, rationale, module mapping, cognitive level, difficulty, source identifiers, applicability context, and credible distractors. Answer keys, rationales, instructor notes, and protected assessment content must remain server side and unavailable to unauthorized learners. The course tutor must be locked from answer disclosure during graded or protected assessments.

## 8. Accessibility contract

Every course must provide captions, transcripts, audio description or an approved equivalent, reduced motion alternatives, keyboard or nonpointer alternatives, readable hierarchy, color independent meaning, alternate descriptions for instructional visuals, and quality control evidence. Accessibility work is a required production role and may not be deferred until after publication.

## 9. Certificate contract

The only credential authorized by this contract is a certificate of course completion. It is not professional certification, licensure, accreditation, compliance evidence, regulatory approval, or authorization to practice. Each certificate package must define issuance criteria, verification fields, transcript fields, unique certificate identification, revocation conditions, retention requirements, and the required disclaimer.

Certificate issuance must be tied to verified entitlement, completion of required lessons and activities, achievement of the passing score, assessment integrity, and successful server side completion processing. Certificate review may be staged without a purchase only for authorized owner review. Production issuance requires the governed learner workflow.

## 10. Compliance staging and publication separation

A course may enter compliance staging only after the protected course package satisfies the structural instructional, assessment, reference, applicability, cinematic planning, accessibility, rights planning, certificate, security, and governance schema. Compliance staging does not authorize publication.

Publication remains blocked until exact references are independently verified, final media is mastered, rights are cleared, accessibility deliverables are accepted, assessments are reviewed, certificates are verified, entitlement and payment behavior is tested, security and privacy controls are validated, backup and rollback evidence exists, all required reviews are approved, and the owner explicitly accepts the exact staged learner experience.

The `publishToAcademy` flag must remain false and checkout must remain unavailable until that final decision. No skipped, failed, stale, synthetic, or partial gate may be treated as a pass.

## 11. Protected checkpoints and failure handling

Every generated package must be checkpointed in the protected Academy PostgreSQL boundary when checkpoint persistence is required. Checkpoints must bind course identity, manifest hash, authoring policy version, production contract version, provider, model, package hash, review status, and organization identity. A checkpoint does not confer approval.

Provider authentication, provider quota, database authentication, checkpoint persistence, schema validation, and integrity failures are fail closed. Retriable failures may use bounded retry and backoff. Nonretriable portfolio failures must stop queued work rather than create untraceable or incomplete packages.

## 12. Command Center separation

The Owner Command Center is a separate parallel workstream. It may display the 36 worker allocation, course progress, compliance staging counts, blockers, and evidence. It may initiate only governed actions through approved local or authenticated interfaces. It does not gain publication authority from this contract and must not infer live status from placeholder data.

Endpoint installation, connector authentication, device registration, encrypted credential storage, update, uninstall, recovery, and live monitoring require direct verification on the owner endpoint. A packaged installer alone is not proof that the Command Center is installed or operational.

## 13. Required evidence

The surge must emit machine readable evidence for worker allocation, worker roles, course assignments, provider preflight, database bootstrap, checkpoint restoration, package generation, retries, failures, source verification status, applicability mapping, narrative depth, assessment counts, cinematic scene and shot counts, accessibility plans, rights plans, certificate plans, compliance staging, publication blockers, LCMS loading, and owner acceptance.

All evidence must preserve the distinction between source completion, structural validation, compliance staging, final release readiness, publication approval, learner availability, and commercial operation.
